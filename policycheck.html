    4 Payments over time

    For a payment that repeats every year (a dividend, a grant, a stipend, a wage supplement). Each year is placed against the year before, because last year's payment is what a household budgets on. Then the same history is run through two payout rules to see which one keeps every year out of Shatter, and how big a reserve that takes.

    One year per line. The third number is optional: what the formula or the law said the payment should be. When it is there, the year is also placed against it (paid versus promised).

    Years

    Each year pays the average of the last N years' amounts.

    Max drop %
    Max rise %

    Each year pays the amount, but never less than last year minus the max drop, and never more than last year plus the max rise. Good years put the difference into a reserve; lean years draw it down. A max drop of 12.0% sits just under the IVA Peak edge (TL_IVA = 12.047%).

    Window years

    Each year is also placed against the highest payment of the previous N years, so a slow slide that never trips the year-to-year check is still caught. To guarantee no N-year stretch reaches Shatter, the max drop must stay below 1 − (1 − TL)^(1/N): 4.79% per year for 3 years.

      How the check works

      The measure. Each group's torsion is its adverse change against its own reference, the reduction of the Applied Identity Physics Economics paper: τ = |X − X_ref| / X_ref, counted only in the direction that harms the group. X_ref is the group's situation before the policy; X is the projected situation after. A group that ends up better off has τ = 0 and is shown as a gain.

      The placement. TL = 0.136899099984016 and TL_IVA = 0.88 × TL = 0.120471207985934. A group below TL_IVA is Locked: it carries the change. Between TL_IVA and TL it is at IVA Peak: under strain at the edge. At or above TL it is in Shatter: the change exceeds what the group can carry. The Economics paper places real events the same way: a 2% inflation target at τ = 0.020 (Locked), US output in 1932 at 0.129 (IVA Peak), a 20% bear market at 0.200 (Shatter).

      Bounded uncertainty. The ± band is applied to each projection. If the whole band stays inside one phase, the placement holds. If the band reaches a boundary, the placement is marked open, and better data decides it.

      The verdict. The policy passes when no group reaches Shatter at any horizon, either as written or on the ground. The net effect sums each group's signed change weighted by its people: Justice as a net gain across every party (APPA NOHARM Lossless Kernel [9,0,1,1]).

      Payments over time. A repeating payment is placed year against year: τ = 1 − this year ÷ last year, counted only when it drops. The same history is then run through a rolling average and through a bounded-change rule with a reserve. A bounded rule with a max drop below TL can never place a single year in Shatter; the question it answers is how large a reserve that takes, given the real history. Each year is also placed against the highest payment of the previous N years, because a run of drops that each pass can add up to Shatter: the deferred-harm check from Function over Fallacy, applied to the payout rule itself. Smoothing rules of this kind are standard for endowments; Yale's spends 80% of the prior year's spending plus 20% of a target rate on the endowment, for intergenerational neutrality.

      The four checks come from Function over Fallacy: the aggregate mask (the overall number improves while a group is harmed), deferred harm (fine now, Shatter later), compliant on paper (fine as written, Shatter on the ground), and stated allegiance (the named beneficiary is harmed).

      This tool measures effect, not motive. It does not render a verdict on any person, party or intention, and a policy can fail through honest miscalculation. Its output is a starting point for scrutiny, to be confirmed by more than one independent party with real data. The module examples use illustrative numbers; replace them with your own.
