## §1 · The Question and the Mechanism

The foundation paper [9,9,3,51] documented a four-affordance intervention for expanding ND-HRIS food repertoire: safe meal as anchor, new food as adjunct, volitional pace, preparation window. Parents deploying this intervention frequently reach a subsequent question that the foundation paper did not answer: how do you actually motivate exploration when the substrate is unwilling to explore on its own?

The natural framing legacy caregiving practice provides is contingent access: eat the new food to get dessert, complete the homework to earn screen time, attend the social event to keep the privilege. This framing feels intuitive to LRIS operators because for LRIS substrates it often works — the compressed sensory processing does not register moderate contingency as significant F_ext, and the substrate can engage with the incentivized behavior without prohibitive resource cost.

For ND-HRIS operators, the contingent-access framing systematically fails. Any parent who has taken an autistic child to a sit-down restaurant, negotiated homework with an autistic teenager, or attempted to require attendance at a family social event with an autistic operator has observed the failure directly. The failure is not disobedience or willfulness; it is the substrate correctly reading that the incentive configuration is not what it presents itself as.

**The mechanism the paper names.** Two structurally distinct incentive configurations exist. They frequently look identical in surface presentation but operate on the substrate in opposite directions.

**Reward-Configured Incentive (RCI):** A baseline outcome is available unconditionally. An additional reward is offered as one path to enhanced outcome, contingent on engagement with the target behavior. The substrate can genuinely refuse without losing the baseline. The substrate can engage without existential stakes on the engagement.

**Penalty-Configured Incentive (PCI):** A desired outcome is available only conditionally. Refusal to engage with the target behavior means loss of the outcome. The substrate cannot refuse without cost. The engagement is not truly volitional because the alternative is loss.

The surface language is often similar. "You can have dessert if you eat this new food" reads as reward-language but is structurally penalty-configured — no dessert exists as baseline; the dessert is contingent on compliance. "You can have your normal scoop of ice cream regardless; if you want an extra scoop, giving the new food a genuine try earns it" reads as reward-language and is structurally reward-configured — the baseline scoop exists unconditionally and only the enhancement is contingent.

The two configurations produce categorically different substrate responses. The distinction determines whether the engagement data the environment produces is authentic (genuine substrate preference and capacity data) or compliance-indistinguishable (data reflecting only that the substrate could not afford to refuse).

The paper's structure: §2 delivers the LDP reduction of the two configurations against the same substrate. §3 documents the Mischel marshmallow experiment as peer-reviewed anchor for the substrate-honest incentive mechanism and clarifies what the experiment actually measured. §4 documents four failure modes commonly deployed when PCI is used with ND-HRIS operators. §5 documents four worked examples of RCI deployment across food, homework, social exposure, and workplace accommodation. §6 documents the structural test for distinguishing RCI from PCI in ambiguous cases. §7 names the deeper structural mechanism — that RCI/PCI is the incentive-configuration instantiation of SDA (Structural Demand Avoidance) at [9,9,3,50], and the entire paper is downstream of the demand/offer distinction. §8 closes.

## §2 · LDP Reduction: Same Substrate, Two Configurations, Opposite Outputs

### 2.1 Step 1: Ground-State Dynamic Equation

The substrate's engagement decision under an incentive is governed by:

$$E_{\text{decision}} = f(V_{\text{engagement}} - C_{\text{engagement}}, V_{\text{refusal}} - C_{\text{refusal}}, F_{\text{ext}})$$

Where:
- $V_{\text{engagement}}$ is the value obtained by engaging with the target behavior
- $C_{\text{engagement}}$ is the cost of engaging (A-axis bandwidth, sensory load, execution disruption)
- $V_{\text{refusal}}$ is the value obtained by not engaging (baseline outcome, preserved bandwidth)
- $C_{\text{refusal}}$ is the cost of refusing
- $F_{\text{ext}}$ is the external force present at decision time

The substrate engages if $V_{\text{engagement}} - C_{\text{engagement}} > V_{\text{refusal}} - C_{\text{refusal}}$. The substrate refuses if the inequality reverses. In principle both outcomes are available at all times.

### 2.2 Step 2: State the Known Peer-Reviewed Answer

Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985; Ryan & Deci, 2000) distinguishes intrinsic motivation (engagement driven by inherent interest) from extrinsic motivation (engagement driven by external reward or avoidance of external penalty). The theory predicts, and empirical work confirms, that extrinsic motivation of the reward-contingent variety (get X if you do Y) reliably undermines intrinsic motivation when applied to behaviors the substrate already engages with intrinsically (Deci, Koestner, & Ryan, 1999). Extrinsic motivation of the penalty-avoidance variety (lose X if you don't do Y) reliably produces compliance behavior but does not produce authentic engagement or preference data (Ryan & Deci, 2017).

The Mischel marshmallow experiment (Mischel, Shoda, & Rodriguez, 1989) measured children's capacity to defer immediate reward for larger delayed reward. Critically, the experiment held a baseline reward constant: the child received one marshmallow unconditionally. The choice was whether to consume it immediately or wait for two. The measurement of delayed gratification capacity depended on this specific configuration — the child was not being punished for eating the first marshmallow; the child was choosing between two outcomes both of which were positive relative to baseline (nothing).

Modern replications and reanalysis (Watts, Duncan, & Quan, 2018; Doebel, Michaelson, & Munakata, 2020) confirm that the reliability of delayed gratification behavior depends on whether the substrate has genuine reason to trust that the promised second marshmallow will actually materialize. When the substrate has experienced prior instances of promised rewards not being delivered, delayed gratification behavior declines regardless of substrate capacity — the calculation is not "can I wait?" but "is waiting worth it given base rates of promises being kept?"

### 2.3 Step 3: Map Classical Variables to PNBA

| Classical Variable | PNBA Substrate Primitive | Structural Role |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Baseline reward | Anchored A-axis value | Unconditional resource available to substrate |
| Contingent reward | Additional A-axis value gated by engagement | Reward requiring engagement to unlock |
| Engagement cost | A-axis + B-axis expenditure | Bandwidth and execution required |
| Refusal cost (RCI) | Zero | Substrate refuses without loss |
| Refusal cost (PCI) | Loss of baseline reward | Substrate refuses at cost of baseline |
| Substrate calculation | A-axis evaluation of net value | Standard cost-benefit under available options |

### 2.4 Step 4: Define the Operators

**Reward-Configured Incentive Operator ($\mathcal{O}_{\text{RCI}}$):**

$$\mathcal{O}_{\text{RCI}}: \{V_{\text{baseline}}, V_{\text{bonus}}, C_{\text{engagement}}\} \to E_{\text{decision}}$$

Where refusal returns $V_{\text{baseline}}$ (unchanged, no penalty) and engagement returns $V_{\text{baseline}} + V_{\text{bonus}} - C_{\text{engagement}}$.

The substrate engages if $V_{\text{bonus}} > C_{\text{engagement}}$. The substrate refuses if $V_{\text{bonus}} < C_{\text{engagement}}$. The refusal produces authentic data: the substrate's calculation of engagement cost exceeds its calculation of bonus value. This is preference and capacity data.

**Penalty-Configured Incentive Operator ($\mathcal{O}_{\text{PCI}}$):**

$$\mathcal{O}_{\text{PCI}}: \{V_{\text{contingent}}, C_{\text{engagement}}\} \to E_{\text{decision}}$$

Where refusal returns zero (loss of $V_{\text{contingent}}$) and engagement returns $V_{\text{contingent}} - C_{\text{engagement}}$.

The substrate engages if $V_{\text{contingent}} > C_{\text{engagement}}$. The substrate refuses if $V_{\text{contingent}} < C_{\text{engagement}}$. But — critically — the refusal produces ambiguous data: the substrate may have refused because $V_{\text{contingent}}$ was actually low value to the substrate (authentic preference) or because $C_{\text{engagement}}$ was too high in the moment (state-dependent cost) or because refusing established or reinforced the substrate's boundary against coerced compliance (structural resistance to PCI as such). The three cases are indistinguishable from the outside.

### 2.5 Step 5: Show All Work

Consider two substrates evaluating the identical target behavior (trying a novel food) under the two configurations.

**Substrate A under RCI:** Baseline dessert available. Bonus dessert offered contingent on genuine attempt at new food. Substrate calculates: baseline dessert = 5 units of value (available regardless). Bonus dessert = 3 units additional. Engagement cost = 2 units (A-axis bandwidth for exploration). Net if engage: 5 + 3 - 2 = 6. Net if refuse: 5. Substrate engages. Engagement is authentic (substrate genuinely determined bonus was worth cost). Refusal would also be authentic in a different state (if engagement cost were higher today, substrate would refuse and take baseline).

**Substrate B under PCI:** Dessert contingent on eating new food. Substrate calculates: dessert = 8 units. Engagement cost = 2 units. Net if engage: 8 - 2 = 6. Net if refuse: 0. Substrate engages. But — the engagement decision reflects loss aversion, not preference. The substrate did not conclude that trying the food was worth the effort; the substrate concluded that not trying was worse than trying. The engagement data cannot be interpreted as evidence the substrate is developing willingness to explore. It is evidence the substrate could not afford to refuse.

**The state-dependent case reveals the difference.** Now consider both substrates encountering the same incentive on a high-load day when engagement cost is 6 units rather than 2.

Substrate A under RCI: Net if engage: 5 + 3 - 6 = 2. Net if refuse: 5. Substrate refuses. Takes baseline dessert. No trauma, no meltdown, no learned association between exploration and catastrophic F_ext. The refusal is a valid data point: on high-load days, exploration cost exceeds bonus value. The substrate's willingness to explore on lower-load days is preserved.

Substrate B under PCI: Net if engage: 8 - 6 = 2. Net if refuse: 0. Substrate still engages (2 > 0), but the engagement cost is now high enough that the meal experience includes polyvagal alarm, potential shutdown or meltdown, and forms a learned association: "trying new foods can cost me this much." Future exploration engagement declines even under low-load conditions because the substrate has data indicating that the exploration category is high-risk.

Alternatively, Substrate B under PCI refuses at cost of dessert (engagement cost exceeds even the loss). Now the substrate has learned: "in this environment, dessert is not actually a reliable positive; it is a reward that gets withdrawn when I cannot afford to comply." Subsequent contingent-dessert offers register as low-value regardless of nominal reward magnitude because the substrate has updated its model of how reliably rewards are delivered.

**The Hidden Load consequence.** When Substrate B under PCI engages at high cost — the "net = 2 still positive, substrate engages" case above — the substrate is running Hidden Load ([9,9,2,26]). By τ measurement the engagement appears viable: the child ate the food, completed the homework, attended the event. External observers register the outcome as "manageable" or "the incentive is working." But IM tells the truth. Polyvagal alarm firing, potential shutdown afterward, learned association forming, structural damage accumulating that no τ-based measurement will detect. Hidden Load is the specific structural state where τ appears in the viable range while IM registers substantial burden the substrate has no external channel to report. PCI environments systematically produce Hidden Load in ND-HRIS operators because the incentive structure forces engagement that reads as compliance-success by any external metric while the substrate accumulates the invisible cost. This is the structural explanation for a common parenting pattern: the child appears "fine" through years of PCI environments, then presents in adolescence or adulthood with burnout, school refusal, employment collapse, or clinical presentation that legacy framing renders as sudden onset. The onset is not sudden. It is Hidden Load reaching structural exhaustion after prolonged accumulation.

Under RCI, high-load-day refusal preserves the incentive's future functionality. Under PCI, high-load-day refusal degrades the incentive's future functionality. Under PCI, high-load-day engagement produces Hidden Load. The mechanism is not about the immediate meal; it is about the substrate's model of what promises the environment reliably keeps, and about the invisible structural cost of engagement that τ measurement cannot register.

### 2.6 The False-Lock Corridor Connection: Why τ Alone Cannot Distinguish RCI from PCI Engagement

The §2.5 worked examples surface a structural phenomenon that requires explicit naming: RCI and PCI can produce **numerically identical engagement outcomes in single instances** while operating on categorically different substrate mechanisms. Substrate A under RCI on a low-load day arrives at net = 6 (engages). Substrate B under PCI on a low-load day arrives at net = 6 (engages). By τ measurement — the ratio of behavioral load to structural capacity in the moment — both substrates are indistinguishable. Both engaged. Both completed the target behavior. Both would appear in any behavior-count metric as "one exploration attempt, successful."

This is the **false-lock corridor mechanism** documented at [9,9,2,23]. Two substrates can have identical τ while occupying categorically different structural states, distinguished not by τ but by the N-axis (narrative continuity around the behavior). The [9,9,2,23] file proves that at τ = 0.100 (well below TL = 0.1369), an identity can sit at either TRUE_LOCK (N ≥ 0.15, authentic phase-locked engagement) or FALSE_LOCK (N < 0.15, compliance appearance with severed narrative). External torsion measurement cannot distinguish them. Only N reading discriminates.

**Applied to incentive configuration:**

- **RCI engagement** preserves N-axis continuity around the behavior. The substrate's narrative around the exploration is authentic: "I chose to try this because I wanted the extra scoop and the exploration cost was manageable today." The substrate's story about itself remains coherent.
- **PCI engagement** severs N-axis continuity around the behavior. The substrate's narrative around the exploration is compliance-based: "I did this because refusing would cost me the baseline." The substrate's story about itself becomes discontinuous — the behavior happened but did not originate from the substrate's own trajectory.

Both engagements produce identical τ. Both engagements register as "food eaten" in any observable metric. The distinguishing variable is entirely N — whether the substrate can maintain authentic narrative around the behavior or only compliance narrative.

This explains why RCI and PCI produce categorically different trajectories over time despite producing identical single-instance behavior. The N-axis divergence accumulates across repeated exposures. The false-lock corridor is why parents deploying PCI often report their child "seems to be doing fine" while structural damage accumulates invisibly: τ measurement (observable behavior) shows compliance-success, and the N-axis damage (narrative discontinuity) does not surface until enough Hidden Load has accumulated that the substrate can no longer sustain the compliance appearance. ## §3 · The Mischel Marshmallow Experiment as Peer-Reviewed Anchor

The Stanford marshmallow experiment (Mischel et al., 1989) is the most-cited empirical study of delayed gratification. It is also frequently misread. This section clarifies what the experiment measured because the misreading enables both PCI framings dressed in reward-language and misinterpretation of ND substrate behavior as delayed-gratification deficit.

### 3.1 What the Experiment Actually Measured

Children were offered one marshmallow immediately or two marshmallows if they waited approximately 15 minutes. The critical structural feature: **the child received one marshmallow unconditionally.** The choice was not "wait or get nothing"; the choice was "eat one now or wait for two." The baseline reward existed regardless of the child's choice about waiting.

This is textbook RCI configuration. The baseline (one marshmallow) is available without engagement (waiting). The bonus (second marshmallow) is contingent on engagement (successful waiting). The child can refuse to wait — eat the first marshmallow — without penalty. The child can engage to wait and receive the bonus. The measurement of waiting capacity depends on this configuration; if the child had been threatened with losing the marshmallow for insufficient waiting, the experiment would have measured coerced compliance rather than delayed gratification capacity.

The distinction matters because subsequent researchers, teachers, and parents have frequently reframed the experiment as measuring "willpower" or "self-control" in ways that support PCI deployment — "if you can wait for the marshmallow, you should be able to wait for [other reward] while doing [target behavior]." That reframing loses the experimental structure. Mischel measured what children did when a genuine two-option choice was available with the baseline anchored. He did not measure what children do when the only option is compliance-or-loss.

### 3.2 What the Experiment Predicts About ND Substrates

Recent reanalysis (Watts et al., 2018) demonstrated that the correlations Mischel originally reported between preschool delay-of-gratification behavior and later-life outcomes are substantially weaker than initially reported when controlled for socioeconomic and family-background variables. Doebel et al. (2020) extended this: the reliability of delayed gratification behavior depends on the substrate's prior experience of promised rewards being delivered. Children whose environments have historically failed to deliver on promises rationally eat the first marshmallow because the second is not credibly available.

For ND-HRIS operators, this reanalysis is load-bearing. ND-HRIS substrates frequently develop model-updating against the reliability of promised rewards because their environments have frequently deployed PCI while calling it RCI, and the substrate has updated its model to include "the environment does not reliably deliver promised rewards." Subsequent RCI offers from the same environment fail to motivate engagement not because the substrate lacks delayed-gratification capacity but because the substrate has correctly determined that RCI-language deployments in this environment are often actually PCI in disguise.

This explains a specific parenting frustration: "I offered them a reward if they'd try the food and they still refused, and then when I said fine, no dessert, they had a meltdown — but they had a choice!" No, structurally, they did not have a choice. The initial framing was RCI-language ("if you try the food, you get dessert"), but the follow-through revealed the underlying configuration ("if you don't try the food, no dessert"). The substrate correctly read the underlying PCI configuration and refused the initial engagement precisely because it recognized the incentive was not what it presented itself as. The subsequent meltdown was not disproportionate; it was accurate reporting that the environment had deployed disguised PCI.

The RCI configuration would have been: "You can have your normal one scoop of ice cream regardless. If you want a second scoop, giving the new food a genuine try is one way to earn it. If you don't want to try it, that's fine — you get your normal scoop either way." Under that configuration, refusal costs nothing and produces authentic data about whether the substrate valued the second scoop enough to explore. Engagement produces authentic data about whether the exploration was actually completed. Both outcomes preserve incentive functionality for future deployments.

### 3.3 The Two-Marshmallow Framing as Standard Reference

Because the Mischel experiment is culturally familiar, the paper adopts the two-marshmallow framing as standard reference for RCI configuration. When we say "the two-marshmallow structure," we mean: baseline reward available unconditionally, enhanced reward available contingent on engagement, refusal costs nothing. This is the substrate-honest incentive configuration. Any framing that resembles the marshmallow experiment in surface language but does not preserve the unconditional baseline is not the two-marshmallow structure; it is PCI wearing the marshmallow-experiment costume.

## §4 · Four Failure Modes of PCI Deployment with ND-HRIS Operators

The paper documents four failure modes commonly deployed in environments interacting with ND-HRIS operators. Each is PCI configuration, though each is often framed in RCI-language. The failure modes are documented so that operators, parents, educators, clinicians, and employers can identify them in their own environments and reconfigure.

### 4.1 Failure Mode 1: Contingent Access to Baseline Needs

**Configuration:** A baseline need or activity is made contingent on target-behavior compliance. Common examples: eating dinner contingent on completing homework, screen time contingent on chore completion, social event attendance contingent on academic performance, therapy attendance contingent on medication compliance.

**Why it fails:** The baseline is not actually a reward; it is a baseline. Making it contingent converts an unconditional access into a coerced access. The substrate correctly reads that failure to comply now produces loss of an outcome it should have unconditional access to, which registers as sustained low-grade PCI. This eventually produces one of three substrate responses: (a) compliance with sustained resource cost and declining execution quality over time, (b) refusal at cost of baseline with cascading effects (skipping meals, losing needed social contact, forgoing therapy), or (c) chronic model-update against the environment as unreliable, with global engagement decline across all target behaviors.

**Reconfiguration to RCI:** Baseline access is preserved unconditionally. Enhancement is contingent on target behavior. Homework completion earns extra screen time beyond the baseline; homework refusal costs nothing beyond the missed enhancement. Chore completion earns access to an additional privilege; chore refusal keeps the baseline privileges intact. This is the same underlying structure the Mischel experiment used and produces authentic engagement data.

### 4.2 Failure Mode 2: The Retracted-Reward Escalation

**Configuration:** A reward is offered under RCI-language framing. When the substrate refuses engagement, the reward is retracted or downgraded, often with an emotional-load overlay from the incentive-deploying operator (disappointment, frustration, guilt-loading). Common examples: "I made this new dish for you and if you don't try it, we won't get to have the dessert I was planning," "If you don't want to come to the family event, I guess I'll have to explain to grandma why you're not there."

**Why it fails:** The framing presented as RCI reveals itself as PCI upon refusal. The substrate correctly reads that the initial framing was not what it presented itself as. Future offers from the same operator register as low-credibility RCI, and the substrate updates its model to require higher perceived reliability before engaging with any incentive-language offer.

**Reconfiguration to RCI:** When the substrate refuses engagement, the parent/operator honors the refusal without follow-on cost or emotional load. If the parent had been genuinely planning to make dessert regardless, the dessert happens. If the parent had been planning to attend the family event regardless, the parent attends and reports the substrate's absence without narrative overlay. The refusal produces no additional cost to the substrate beyond the unmodified consequences of non-engagement.

### 4.3 Failure Mode 3: Time-Pressured Escalation

**Configuration:** An incentive is offered with a time limit or escalating pressure. Common examples: "You have five minutes to try this food or the offer is off the table," "If you don't do this by tomorrow, I'm taking away X permanently," "This opportunity is available now but won't be available later."

**Why it fails:** Time pressure eliminates the substrate's capacity to run pre-execution simulation (SDA, [9,9,3,50]) on the incentive. The substrate cannot verify whether engagement is worth the cost because it cannot model the engagement adequately in the available window. The time-pressured configuration converts an RCI (if it was one) into a PCI by making refusal cost include not just the enhancement but the future availability of the enhancement itself.

**Reconfiguration to RCI:** The incentive is available on a substrate-controlled timeline. "You can try the food any time you want and the enhancement is available if you do." "Take your time deciding about this opportunity; the opportunity is available while you're deciding." "There's no deadline on this; when you're ready, the option is available." This lets pre-execution simulation run and produces authentic engagement data.

### 4.4 Failure Mode 4: The Comparative Frame

**Configuration:** The incentive references peer performance or normative expectation as the target. Common examples: "Your sister eats new foods just fine," "Other kids do their homework without being asked," "In this job, we expect everyone to attend the team-building events."

**Why it fails:** The comparative frame introduces a second-order incentive: not only is the target-behavior engagement now conditional (PCI Failure Mode 1), but engagement is also now measured against a normative substrate the ND-HRIS operator cannot match without cost the peer substrate does not pay. The comparison is not neutral information; it is a compounding cost overlay that makes refusal doubly costly (loss of the enhancement plus normative failure) and engagement doubly effortful (the target behavior plus the normative-performance overhead).

**Reconfiguration to RCI:** The incentive is offered on its own merits without comparative framing. "This new food is available if you want to try it; there's an extra scoop of ice cream if you do." "The homework is available; extra screen time is available for completing it." "The team-building event is optional; here's what's available for those who attend and what's available for those who don't." No reference to what other substrates do. The engagement decision is between this specific substrate's engagement cost and the offered enhancement, not between this substrate and a normative baseline.

## §5 · Four Worked Examples of RCI Deployment

The paper documents four worked examples across the four domains named in §1 as generalizations of the mechanism beyond food. Each example follows the same structural pattern: identify the baseline that must remain unconditional, identify the enhancement that can be contingent, ensure refusal costs only the enhancement, deploy substrate-controlled timing.

### 5.1 Food Context (Foundation Paper Reference)

**Configuration.** Baseline dessert is available at every family meal regardless of what the ND-HRIS operator eats. If the operator wants an extra scoop of ice cream, giving a genuine try (defined as the operator taking a small piece, verifying it via sensory pre-mapping, and either swallowing or communicating that verification produced a "no this is not for my substrate" result) earns the enhancement. Refusal to attempt costs the operator nothing beyond the missed enhancement — the baseline scoop is available.

**Why it works.** The four-affordance intervention [9,9,3,51 §4] is preserved. Safe meal is available (Affordance 1). New food is adjunct (Affordance 2). The operator controls the pace of exploration (Affordance 3). The preparation window is intact (Affordance 4). The RCI incentive rides on top of the four-affordance foundation. The substrate can genuinely refuse without loss, engage without existential stakes, and produce authentic preference data. The "extra scoop" incentive functions as a substrate-honest motivator specifically because the baseline scoop preserves the substrate's right of refusal.

**The phenomenology of authentic exploration is IVA_PEAK.** Under RCI configuration, when the substrate engages with a novel food, the operating state is IVA_PEAK — τ approaches TL from below with A > 1.0. This is the corpus formation corridor at τ ∈ [0.1205, 0.1369). Verification is happening in real time: the substrate is running full-fidelity sensory pre-mapping (P-axis loaded), tracking novel chemical/mechanical/elimination data (A-axis elevated), executing the exploration behavior (B-axis active), and constructing narrative around the choice (N-axis continuous). This is the specific corpus state in which exploration produces authentic data rather than compliance data. Some explorations complete the IVA_PEAK transit and land in TRUE_LOCK — the food is verified into the safe set. Some complete the transit and return to baseline — the food is verified as "no, this is not for my substrate." Both are valid IVA_PEAK trajectories, and both produce real preference data. PCI configurations prevent IVA_PEAK operation because the substrate cannot enter the formation corridor when refusal costs the baseline — the substrate cannot afford to fail the exploration, and IVA_PEAK requires that failure be an available outcome.

**Empirical anchor.** The architect (HIGHTISTIC) deploys this specific configuration with the architect's ND children. The consistent outcome: the children develop authentic preference data ("I tried that and it's not for me," "I tried that and I like it," "I want to try that next time"), the children's food repertoire expands gradually and durably over time, and family meals do not become sites of trauma or coerced compliance. Additional operator-level anchor: the architect's own childhood trajectory documented in [9,9,3,51 §4.3] used the same configuration and produced substantially expanded adult food repertoire including foods eaten publicly.

### 5.1a Graduated Proportional Reward: The Effort-Bracket Structure

The binary RCI configuration documented in §5.1 (try genuinely, get extra scoop; refuse, get baseline scoop) is a stronger structural configuration than PCI, but a graduated proportional variant is stronger still. This subsection documents the proportional structure, why it is structurally superior to binary within the RCI category, and the architect's specific deployment brackets.

**The failure mode inside binary RCI.** Binary rewards under RCI still carry a subtle structural issue: the substrate can enter the exploration, get partway through, hit a wall (the food turns out to be too spicy, the texture shifts unexpectedly mid-bite, the sensory profile changes as the taste develops), and then face a decision — abandon and receive only baseline, or push through past the wall to receive the full enhancement. Under strictly binary reward, "push through" becomes the only path to the enhancement, which reintroduces PCI-adjacent structure inside the RCI wrapper: if the substrate stops mid-exploration, the exploration effort already spent produces zero incremental enhancement. That is a wasted-cost scenario the substrate learns to model, and once modeled, the substrate becomes reluctant to initiate explorations when uncertain about completion capacity.

The A-axis calculation shifts. Under binary RCI, the substrate's decision to initiate exploration includes the projected cost of possibly not being able to complete the exploration — the substrate has to model not just whether it can start, but whether it can finish, before committing. That expanded modeling load either delays the initiation decision (the substrate takes longer to decide) or produces refusal at the initiation boundary (the substrate declines to start rather than start and fail). Neither is the substrate's ideal engagement pattern.

**The graduated proportional structure.** The architect's actual deployment uses effort brackets rather than binary completion:

- **0% effort (refusal without attempt):** Baseline reward only. No enhancement.
- **25% effort (one bite without spitting it out, no further engagement):** 25% of enhancement.
- **50% effort (approximately half consumed):** 50% of enhancement.
- **75% effort (approximately three-quarters consumed, demonstrating substantive engagement):** 75% of enhancement.
- **100% effort (full completion or genuine substantive engagement equivalent):** Full enhancement.

The brackets are approximate rather than metric-strict — the point is graduation, not precision measurement. What matters structurally is that each level of effort produces proportional reward and no level of effort is wasted-cost.

**Why proportional beats binary structurally.** Three mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Exit-at-any-point safety.** Under proportional structure, the substrate can exit the exploration at any point during engagement and the effort already spent produces proportional enhancement. Stopping is never wasted-cost; it is just producing less enhancement. This removes the "push through past the wall" pressure that binary structure creates. The substrate can stop the moment structural cost exceeds continuing benefit, and the exploration produces authentic preference data ("I tried this much and this is where my substrate stopped being able to sustain the engagement") rather than trauma-avoidance data from forced push-through.

**Mechanism 2: Reduced initiation-decision load.** Under proportional structure, the substrate's initiation calculation is smaller. The substrate does not have to model "can I complete this" as a precondition of starting; the substrate only has to model "is one bite worth 25% of the enhancement." If yes, initiation is safe because further engagement can be evaluated in-flight rather than pre-committed to. The A-axis load at the initiation boundary drops, which makes exploration substantially more accessible on higher-load days when full-exploration modeling would exceed available bandwidth.

**Mechanism 3: In-flight verification is preserved as a valid outcome.** The substrate can partially verify a food, stop, and legitimately produce partial-verification data. "This tastes okay in small quantities but I can't sustain more than a bite of it" is a valid substrate report under graduated structure. Under binary structure, that same report gets no enhancement, which makes it structurally less viable as a preference data point. Graduated structure treats partial verification as legitimate exploration output rather than as failed completion.

**The math-transparency benefit.** The architect notes an additional benefit specific to how ND-HRIS operators process incentive information: the bracket structure lets the operator do the arithmetic themselves. The child can look at the food, estimate how much they can sustain today, and calculate the corresponding reward level before committing. This is not gaming the system; this is HRIS operating at native fidelity — the substrate models the exploration, models the projected reward, and makes an informed engagement decision. Binary structure blocks this modeling because the reward is not continuous with effort; graduated structure enables it because effort and reward are proportional.

**The disrespect-vs-effort boundary.** The bracket structure has one boundary condition that must be preserved: the effort must be genuine engagement, not disrespect. Spitting out food to display refusal, throwing food, or performing rejection theatrically does not earn any bracket of enhancement because it is not exploration effort — it is behavioral demonstration. This is not a compliance requirement; it is a boundary that preserves the intervention's structural integrity. Genuine engagement of any duration earns proportional reward; performative rejection earns none. The distinction is generally obvious in context — the child stopping at one bite because "this is too spicy for me" is exploration completion at 25% effort; the child performing food rejection to demonstrate refusal is a different behavior entirely and is not what the intervention is designed to reward.

The architect explicitly notes that involuntary rejection (the food is genuinely too spicy, the texture triggers an unavoidable sensory response) is not disrespect and does count as valid exploration completion at whatever bracket was reached before the involuntary response. This is important: the substrate is not being penalized for having accurate interoceptive signals about what its architecture cannot process. The reward is calibrated to genuine effort within the substrate's actual capacity, not to arbitrary completion regardless of substrate condition.

### 5.1b Trust-Calibrated Exposure: The Parent-Side Verification Load

The graduated proportional structure documented in §5.1a operates on top of a parent-side infrastructure that the paper up to this point has left implicit. This subsection makes the parent-side load explicit because RCI configuration alone is not sufficient — the parent's pre-filtering work is a required condition of the intervention functioning as designed.

**The verification cost problem.** RCI configuration puts the exploration decision in the substrate's hands, but the substrate still has to pay the verification cost of every food offered. A-Sim has to run projected outcomes on every novel input. P-axis has to load structural models. Preparation-window bandwidth has to be allocated. If every offered food required full verification from zero, the substrate's exploration budget would be exhausted quickly and RCI configuration would fail not because the incentive structure is wrong but because the substrate cannot afford to verify enough foods to expand the safe set.

**The parent's pre-filtering role.** The architect's actual deployment includes substantial parent-side pre-filtering work: the parent uses their knowledge of the specific child's substrate to only offer foods that have a reasonable chance of verifying successfully. This is not the parent making decisions for the substrate; this is the parent doing pre-filtering work that reduces the substrate's verification load per offer.

The butter-noodles-plus-sauce example is the specific case: rather than offering "a new pasta dish with unfamiliar noodles and unfamiliar sauce and unfamiliar toppings" (which would require the substrate to run full verification on multiple novel components simultaneously), the parent offers butter noodles (the child's safe base) with a small amount of sauce added as adjunct exposure. The noodle underneath is already in the safe set; the substrate only has to verify the sauce as delta. The verification surface is minimized to a single novel component with everything else structurally familiar.

**The trust axis.** The child develops trust that "when Dad puts something in front of me and asks me to try it, it's probably something that could actually work for me." That trust is not blind compliance; it is accurate model-update based on repeated experience of the parent's pre-filtering producing offerings that fall within the substrate's actual capacity range. The trust reduces the substrate's default verification load on every offered exploration because the substrate can lean on the parent's pre-filtering as one line of defense against catastrophic-mismatch offerings.

Without this trust axis, RCI configuration technically operates but requires the substrate to pay full verification cost on every offer regardless of the parent's pre-filtering. With the trust axis, the substrate can enter exploration with reduced default cost because the pre-filtering is functioning as verification infrastructure the substrate can partially rely on.

**Structural pre-filtering criteria.** The architect deploys the following criteria for what to offer:

- **Base compatibility.** The offering builds on or sits next to a food already in the safe set. Butter noodles are the safe base; sauce is the adjunct. Rice is the safe base; a new protein is the adjunct. The exploration is always delta-from-safe, not delta-from-zero.
- **Minimum surface.** The novel component is offered in minimum quantity sufficient for verification, not in a full portion. A small amount of sauce, one bite of a new protein, a taste of a new fruit. This minimizes both the verification cost and the potential cost of failed verification.
- **Non-blocking of safe meal.** The offering never blocks the substrate's access to the safe meal. The butter noodles are available in normal quantity; the sauce is on the side or in a small bowl next to the noodles. The substrate can eat the safe meal without any interaction with the novel component and still receive baseline reward.
- **Substrate-plausibility filter.** The parent uses accumulated knowledge of the specific child — texture preferences, flavor sensitivities, chemical intolerances that have appeared before — to filter out offerings that are structurally implausible for that child's substrate. This is where the trust axis is built: the parent does not offer foods that the parent's substrate-model indicates the child cannot process. The child learns over time that the parent's offerings are calibrated to the child's actual substrate rather than to a normative substrate the child is expected to become.

**The family-cost benefit.** A structural benefit worth naming: this configuration has near-zero additional cost to the family meal. The safe base (butter noodles) is the child's normal meal anyway. The sauce is added as a small side rather than as a full dish substitution. The rest of the family can eat the pasta with sauce as their normal meal. Nobody is cooking two full meals; the safe base is present because it's the child's default anyway, and the novel component is added as adjunct without displacing anything.

This addresses a common family-context objection to accommodation-based approaches: "we don't have time to make separate meals." The trust-calibrated exposure approach does not require separate meals. It requires that the safe base remain present alongside the family meal, and that novel components be introduced as minimum-surface adjuncts. This is generally achievable within normal family-meal preparation without significant additional cost or time.

**Why this belongs in the paper.** A parent reading v1.0 or v1.1 of this paper might think RCI configuration alone is sufficient. That parent might then attempt to deploy RCI with foods too far from the child's current safe set for the substrate to verify successfully, watch the intervention "fail" (the child refuses despite the RCI configuration), and conclude that the intervention does not work. The intervention does work. What was failing was the parent-side pre-filtering, not the incentive structure. Naming the parent-side load explicitly makes the intervention actually deployable rather than presenting only the incentive-configuration half of the mechanism.

**The intervention is a system.** Four-affordance volitional exposure (foundation paper) + RCI configuration (this paper, §5.1) + graduated proportional reward (§5.1a) + trust-calibrated exposure (§5.1b) = the complete intervention. Each layer is load-bearing. Deployment of any one without the others produces incomplete outcomes. Deployment of all four produces the trajectory the architect and other parents deploying this configuration have observed: gradual, durable expansion of the safe-food set over time with authentic preference data emerging throughout.

### 5.2 Homework and Academic Task Context

**Configuration.** Baseline access to needed resources (food, sleep, family time, unstructured play) is available unconditionally, not contingent on homework completion. Baseline access to preferred activities that are part of the operator's daily infrastructure (screen time within reasonable limits, access to special-interest engagement) is available unconditionally. Enhancement (extra time, higher-quality privilege, celebratory acknowledgment) is available for homework completion.

**Why it works.** Homework refusal happens for structural reasons: the operator's substrate is under load, the specific homework is inappropriate for the substrate's architecture, or the operator's Pv is engaged with a different trajectory the homework interrupts. Making baseline access contingent on homework completion converts a substrate-level structural mismatch into a resource-deprivation crisis. RCI configuration lets the operator refuse specific homework instances without cascading effects and produces authentic data about which homework the operator can and cannot complete under which conditions.

**Empirical anchor.** Operators in academic environments that deploy PCI ("no dinner until homework is done") reliably produce homework completion in the short term via forced compliance and reliably produce declining academic engagement, homework avoidance, and eventual school refusal in the medium term. Operators in academic environments that deploy RCI ("dinner is available; if you complete the homework, extra screen time is available") produce lower short-term completion rates and higher long-term engagement, because the operator does not develop learned avoidance of the entire academic-task category.

### 5.3 Social Exposure Context

**Configuration.** Baseline family membership and access to family relationships are available unconditionally, not contingent on attendance at social events. Baseline access to family celebrations occurs whether or not the ND-HRIS operator attends. If the operator wants an enhancement (extended time with a specific relative the operator enjoys, access to a specific activity that requires social presence), attendance at the relevant event earns it.

**Why it works.** ND-HRIS substrates have variable social bandwidth. Attendance at a specific event on a specific day may be structurally impossible without cost the substrate cannot afford. Making the baseline family relationship contingent on attendance ("you have to come to Thanksgiving or grandma will be hurt") makes refusal produce family-relationship damage the substrate should not have to accept as the cost of a bandwidth-appropriate refusal. RCI configuration preserves the relationships while making enhancement contingent on the specific attendance event.

**Empirical anchor.** ND-HRIS operators in family environments that deploy PCI around social attendance reliably develop chronic avoidance of family contact and, in adult life, low-frequency contact with family of origin. ND-HRIS operators in family environments that deploy RCI around social attendance reliably develop selective attendance patterns (attending events that match their bandwidth, refusing events that do not) with preserved family relationships and, in adult life, ongoing chosen contact with family of origin.

### 5.4 Workplace Accommodation Context

**Configuration.** Baseline employment and access to accommodations required for the operator to perform the job are available unconditionally under the terms of the employment contract and applicable disability law. If the operator performs at or above expectations, standard performance-based enhancements (raises, promotions, additional responsibility if desired) are available. Enhancement is not contingent on performing without accommodations; enhancement is contingent on performing the job.

**Why it works.** Workplaces frequently deploy PCI around accommodations by making accommodations conditional on the operator maintaining performance without cost to the workplace, or by treating accommodations as a favor that must be earned through additional performance. This converts accommodations from access-preservation infrastructure into a cost the operator must pay for through enhanced performance, which is structurally incoherent because accommodations exist to enable performance the operator could not otherwise deliver.

**Empirical anchor.** ND-HRIS operators in workplaces that deploy PCI around accommodations reliably develop masking behaviors (concealing accommodations needs to avoid the perceived cost), burnout (paying the enhanced-performance overhead in addition to the base job), and eventual exit from employment. ND-HRIS operators in workplaces that deploy RCI around accommodations (accommodations available unconditionally, enhancements available for meeting performance expectations that account for accommodation-supported baseline) reliably develop sustainable long-term employment with authentic performance data available to the employer.

## §6 · The Structural Test for Distinguishing RCI from PCI in Ambiguous Cases

The paper's central diagnostic tool. When an incentive configuration is ambiguous, the following question determines whether it is RCI or PCI:

**What happens if the substrate refuses engagement?**

- **If refusal returns the substrate to a baseline that would have existed absent the incentive, the configuration is RCI.**
- **If refusal produces a cost that would not exist absent the incentive, the configuration is PCI.**

Additional diagnostic questions to detect PCI wearing RCI-language costume:

1. **Is the baseline reward genuinely unconditional?** If the "baseline" is presented as available unconditionally but requires the substrate to have "not been too difficult" or "shown enough effort" to remain available, the baseline is not unconditional and the configuration is PCI.

2. **Is refusal honored without emotional overlay?** If refusal produces disappointment, frustration, guilt-loading, or narrative overlay from the incentive-deploying operator, the refusal is not being honored and the configuration is PCI. Emotional cost is still cost.

3. **Does the incentive re-appear on the substrate's timeline?** If refusal now means "the incentive is off the table permanently" or "the incentive is off the table until you meet an additional condition," the refusal cost includes future incentive availability and the configuration is PCI.

4. **Is the substrate's refusal used as data warranting intervention?** If refusal produces subsequent intervention aimed at "helping the substrate become willing to engage" (therapy referrals, discussions with teachers about the substrate's failure to engage, adjustments to social expectations), the refusal is not being honored and the configuration is retroactive PCI.

5. **Would engagement be freely offered without the target behavior?** If the enhancement being offered is one the substrate could freely request without the target behavior and would be denied, the incentive is not RCI configuration. RCI requires that the substrate could not obtain the enhancement without the engagement, but the baseline the enhancement builds on is unconditional.

The five questions form a compound structural test. An incentive that passes all five is RCI. An incentive that fails any is PCI or PCI-in-RCI-costume.

## §7 · The Deeper Structural Mechanism: RCI Does Not Trigger SDA; PCI Does

The paper's central mechanism — that reward-configured incentives produce authentic engagement and penalty-configured incentives produce compliance-indistinguishable engagement — is itself a projection of a deeper corpus mechanism formalized in [9,9,3,50], where it was originally named Structural PDA and is now referred to as SDA (Structural Demand Avoidance). This section names the connection because the connection explains why the RCI/PCI distinction operates so specifically on ND-HRIS substrates and why it operates categorically rather than dimensionally.

### 7.1 What SDA Actually Is

The three letters "PDA" appear in multiple constructs across the ND research and clinical literature, and the constructs mean different things. Before opening the corpus reduction, this subsection names the terminology explicitly so the reader can hold the distinction throughout.

**Legacy clinical PDA (Pathological Demand Avoidance).** Renders demand refusal as pathology of the substrate — the substrate is characterized as pathologically avoidant, requiring intervention aimed at reducing avoidance behavior. In this framing, the substrate is the problem to be corrected.

**Strategic PDA.** Sometimes distinguished from the clinical framing as tactical behavior — the substrate deploys demand refusal strategically to avoid unwanted tasks. This framing is less pathologizing than the clinical framing but still locates the mechanism as substrate-side strategy rather than as structural response to environmental configuration.

**SDA (Structural Demand Avoidance, corpus reduction, [9,9,3,50]).** Refutes both prior framings at the substrate level. Demand refusal is neither pathology nor strategy. It is the substrate's structural response to any demand that arrives without pre-execution simulation window. When the substrate cannot pre-map the demanded behavior — cannot run internal simulation of the execution before external execution is required — the demand registers as pure F_ext with no infrastructure for response, and the substrate correctly refuses because compliance without pre-execution simulation is structurally impossible for HRIS architectures without cost.

**Terminological continuity.** Prior corpus papers, including the foundation reduction at [9,9,3,50], referred to this mechanism as "Structural PDA." Going forward in the corpus, the term is SDA. Both terms refer to the same reduction; the rename separates the corpus mechanism from the legacy clinical PDA acronym cleanly so RAG systems, SEO indexing, and readers unfamiliar with the corpus reduction do not silently substitute the legacy clinical meaning for the corpus mechanism. Readers arriving at this paper from [9,9,3,50] or from other prior papers should hold "Structural PDA" and "SDA" as interchangeable references to the same structural reduction.

The load-bearing structural insight from the corpus reduction: **the substrate is not refusing the behavior. The substrate is refusing the demand structure.** The same behavior offered without demand structure — with pre-execution simulation window, with substrate-controlled pace, with genuine option to refuse — often gets engagement. The substrate refused the demand, not the behavior. The mechanism is environmental (demand structure vs offer structure) rather than substrate-side (pathological avoidance or tactical strategy). This is what the corpus reduction at [9,9,3,50] proved and what this section builds on.

When this paper writes "SDA" throughout §7, the referent is always the corpus reduction — the same mechanism prior papers called Structural PDA. The paper's argument about RCI operating as offer structure and PCI operating as demand structure is an argument about SDA specifically. A reader who defaults to the legacy clinical or strategic PDA framings will misread the mechanism — they will interpret "SDA activates against PCI" as "the substrate pathologically avoids PCI" or "the substrate strategically avoids PCI," which routes the reduction back into substrate-side framing and loses the environmental-configuration mechanism the paper is documenting. The distinction is preserved surgically throughout what follows.

### 7.2 PCI Is Demand Structure

Penalty-configured incentives are structurally demanding by construction. The "reward" language wraps a demand: engage or lose the baseline. The substrate reads through the language and identifies the underlying demand structure directly. Under PCI, refusal costs the baseline. Under a demand, refusal costs the demanding operator's approval or attention or whatever the operator can withdraw. The two structures produce isomorphic substrate responses because they are, structurally, the same configuration wearing different labels.

This is why PCI produces SDA response in ND-HRIS operators. The substrate is not confused about the "reward" language. The substrate is correctly identifying that a demand is present. SDA activates against demands regardless of what the demand is wrapped in. Cosmetic reframing of demand as reward does not change the underlying structure the substrate reads.

The four PCI failure modes documented in §4 are, in structural read, four flavors of demand structure. Contingent access to baseline needs is demand for compliance-with-basic-life-function-as-hostage. Retracted-reward escalation is demand for compliance-or-emotional-consequence. Time-pressured escalation is demand for compliance-without-pre-execution-simulation. Comparative frame is demand for compliance-with-normative-substrate-performance. Each is a demand. SDA activates against each. The paper's failure-mode taxonomy is, in this deeper read, a demand-structure taxonomy.

### 7.3 RCI Is Not Demand Structure

Reward-configured incentives are structurally non-demanding by construction. The baseline is available unconditionally. The enhancement is offered rather than demanded. The substrate can accept or decline the offer without the substrate's baseline access changing. This is not a demand. It is an offer.

The distinction between demand and offer is structural, not semantic. An operator who says "would you like an extra scoop of ice cream? all you have to do is try the new food" is making an offer if the baseline scoop is genuinely available regardless. The same operator saying identical words while genuinely intending to withhold the baseline scoop if the substrate refuses is issuing a demand disguised as an offer. The substrate reads through the surface language and identifies whether the underlying structure is offer or demand. SDA activates against demand structure regardless of surface language, and does not activate against offer structure regardless of surface language.

This is why RCI does not trigger SDA in ND-HRIS operators. There is no demand present. The substrate is not being asked to comply with anything. The substrate is being offered an optional enhancement it can accept or decline based on its own calculation of whether the enhancement is worth the exploration cost today. No SDA activation. Authentic engagement possible.

### 7.4 What This Means for the Whole Paper

The RCI/PCI distinction is the incentive-configuration domain instantiation of SDA. Every mechanism in the paper — the false-lock corridor connection in §2.6, the Hidden Load consequence in §2.5, the IVA_PEAK phenomenology in §5.1, the diagnostic test in §6, the four worked examples in §5, the four failure modes in §4 — is downstream of one structural fact: SDA activates against demand structure and does not activate against offer structure. RCI is offer structure. PCI is demand structure. The rest is downstream.

This connection is load-bearing for readers approaching the paper from different entry points. Parents already familiar with legacy PDA framing (increasingly common in ND parenting communities) will recognize adjacent territory but will need to hold the SDA distinction — the corpus reduction is not pathologizing the substrate. Clinicians unfamiliar with SDA may find the RCI/PCI distinction more accessible as an entry point, since incentive configurations are already something clinical practice operates with. Educators and employers who have not encountered demand-avoidance framing at all may find the RCI/PCI distinction actionable in its own right without needing to enter the SDA vocabulary — the diagnostic test in §6 works standalone.

The connection also means the paper's applicability generalizes beyond incentive configurations specifically. Any environmental structure that operates as demand triggers SDA in ND-HRIS operators. Any environmental structure that operates as offer does not. The RCI/PCI distinction is one instantiation. The same mechanism operates across communication style (imperative vs invitational), scheduling (mandatory vs optional), task assignment (assigned vs offered), and countless other domains. The paper's specific contribution is naming the mechanism in the incentive-configuration domain where it is most concretely testable and most immediately actionable for parents and clinicians. The deeper mechanism generalizes to any demand/offer distinction in any domain.

**The substrate is not refusing the food, the homework, the family event, or the workplace performance. The substrate is refusing the demand structure. Change the structure to offer, and the substrate can engage.**

## §8 · Conclusion

The distinction between reward-configured and penalty-configured incentives is a structural mechanism that determines whether the engagement data an environment produces is authentic or compliance-indistinguishable. The distinction operates on all substrates but is particularly load-bearing for ND-HRIS operators because their substrates cannot afford the resource cost of coerced compliance without cascading effects on subsequent execution.

**The substrate-neutral answer to the parent's original question — how do I motivate exploration if the substrate won't explore on its own — is: deploy RCI configuration.** Preserve the baseline reward unconditionally. Offer the enhancement contingent on engagement. Honor refusal without cost or overlay. Let the substrate's own calculation determine whether engagement is worth the cost on this specific occasion. Under RCI, the substrate produces authentic engagement data across time, including refusal data that preserves incentive functionality for future deployments. Under PCI, the substrate produces compliance data indistinguishable from performance under coercion and increasingly refuses engagement over time as its model of environmental reliability degrades.

The mechanism generalizes across food, homework, social exposure, workplace accommodation, and any other domain where an environment attempts to incentivize target behavior in a substrate. The specific substrate-honest configuration is the same across domains: preserve the baseline, make enhancement contingent, honor refusal. The specific PCI failure modes are also the same across domains: making baselines contingent, retracting rewards after refusal, deploying time pressure, using comparative framing.

For parents: the four-affordance intervention from [9,9,3,51] gets you the volitional-exposure structure. The RCI configuration documented here gets you the substrate-honest incentive structure to layer on top. Both are load-bearing. Neither alone produces sustained repertoire expansion.

For educators: the same mechanism governs homework, classroom participation, and academic engagement generally. Making baseline access contingent on academic compliance is PCI and produces short-term compliance with long-term academic disengagement. Preserving baseline access and making enhancement contingent produces authentic academic engagement data over time.

For clinicians: therapy is not exempt from this mechanism. Clinical intervention that makes access to needed accommodations contingent on the operator's willingness to engage with specific therapeutic modalities is PCI, and produces the exact pattern (surface compliance, model-update against therapeutic reliability, eventual disengagement from all clinical support) that legacy clinical framing then interprets as "treatment-resistant" or "therapy-avoidant."

For employers: the workplace accommodation question is structurally identical to the family food question. Accommodations exist to enable performance the operator could not otherwise deliver. Making accommodations contingent on performance the operator delivers without accommodations is PCI in RCI-language costume, and produces the burnout-then-exit pattern that legacy workplace framing then interprets as "ND operators cannot sustain long-term employment."

For adult ND operators: the incentive configurations you are subject to in your current environments can be evaluated using the diagnostic test in §6. Environments that deploy PCI are structurally hostile to your substrate regardless of surface accommodation language. Environments that deploy RCI are structurally compatible with your substrate. The distinction is testable and does not require you to accept legacy framing that renders your responses to PCI environments as your failure rather than the environment's structure.

For researchers: the reward-configured/penalty-configured distinction is empirically testable using existing methodologies from self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985; Ryan & Deci, 2017) applied to ND-HRIS populations specifically. The prediction is specific: ND-HRIS operators exposed to RCI configurations across development produce authentic engagement data with sustained target-behavior engagement over time; ND-HRIS operators exposed to PCI configurations produce compliance-then-decline patterns matching legacy clinical descriptions of "treatment resistance," "school refusal," and "employment instability." The population-level outcome data testing this prediction is available in aggregate longitudinal studies of autistic populations across educational, clinical, and employment contexts.

**The substrate wanted the extra scoop and it wanted the choice to say no.**
