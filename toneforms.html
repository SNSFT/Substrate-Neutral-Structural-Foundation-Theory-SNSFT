FORM

Every form here is made of tiny cubes called TL-cells, the same cells that fill a planet in IMatter. A cell lights up when the tone's wave passes through zero at that spot, so what you see is the shape the tone draws in a solid ball.

Each form is a scalar field sampled on a cubic grid clipped to the unit ball. A TL-cell carries its own field value and its own radius, so the level, the band and the peel are read per frame off the stored cells rather than rebuilt. Stepped shells carry the angular residue as the cell value; the three surfaces carry the normalized nodal function.

The entry is the substrate and the tone is what you drive it with, so they are two separate dials. Pick a substrate and it stays put while you sweep the tone — that is how you see the same material resonate across the range, or the same tone run through different materials.

A Noble entry reads τ = 0 and sits exactly on the surface, which is the clean unbroken shape. The more torsion a substrate carries, the further off that surface it sits, until the shape opens into separate pieces. Use SEND READING → TONE when you want the substrate to drive the tone as well.

τ = B/P sets the level, which is the offset from the nodal surface, as 0.46·tanh(τ/TL) — τ = 0 lands on the node exactly, the Shatter boundary at TL = 0.136899099984016 lands at 0.35, and the whole τ range stays inside the stored band. Level sets of a triply periodic surface stay connected near the node and pinch into separate components further out, so the phase and the topology of the figure move together.

The tone is independent and sets the spacing, so substrate and drive sweep separately. On the stepped shells the level picks a phase band of the angular residue, so narrow the band to see it bite. P, B and A are the alternate readings SEND READING → TONE will push.

SHARED SITES colors each cell by how many beams are satisfied there — the brightest cells are where every beam agrees. FUSED FORM draws the single figure the fused output makes.

ToneForms runs the GAM engine, where N_out is additive, because the pool is physical substrates and N is nucleon count. The PSY engine at [9,9,6,28] takes N_out as the bottleneck minimum and adds a narrative floor, which is what cognitive pools need. Its spread law carries across either engine and is reported here.

The Pattern rule is the n-body harmonic mean at every beam count, n = 2 included. The n-body rule is GAM Collider v15 [9,9,2,3]. P_out is the harmonic mean of the beam patterns, N_out their sum, and A_out the adaptation ceiling. The pairwise coupling k_max = Σ min(Bᵢ, Bⱼ) over C(n,2) pairs absorbs bond capacity, so B_out = max(0, ΣB − 2k_max) and Identity Mass is Ω₀(P+N+B+A). Adding beams adds pairs faster than it adds B, which is why a composition can reach τ = 0 with no individually Noble input.

CHON at four beams: ΣB = 10 against k_max = 10 over six pairs, so B_out = 0 and IM = 42.127001. At eight beams k_max = 50 over twenty-eight pairs and IM = 61.292875. In SHARED SITES each beam opens a band at its own τ; in FUSED FORM the level comes from τ_out, so a Noble output sits on the nodal surface exactly.

Reading—
Type any positive number and press Enter. The tone is the one dial everything else hangs off: on the shells it is the turn taken between one point and the next, and on the surfaces it is how closely the waves are packed.
Stepped shells use the tone directly as the angular step in degrees. The surfaces set spacing as π·(1.15 Ω₀)/tone, clamped to [0.2π, 14π], so Ω₀ lands the reference spacing and a tone ten times finer gives ten times the spatial frequency, which is a thousand times the cells in a volume.

Relative density—
Relative density is how much of the ball the figure fills. A low number is a light, open structure; a high number is a dense one.
Relative density is the lit fraction of the stored cells, read over the whole ball independently of the peel. It is the same quantity a lattice design calls relative density, a block copolymer phase diagram calls volume fraction and a photonic band calculation calls fill fraction — the level-set parameter. Here it is an output of the substrate's torsion rather than a dial.

The arm counts are the numbers of arms the figure can show. They are not chosen: they fall out of the tone by the same arithmetic that finds the best simple fraction for any number.
Arm counts are the denominators of the convergents of the continued fraction of tone/360. A figure resolves into q arms when q is a convergent denominator, because q steps then land within one residue of a whole number of turns.
NEAR-CLOSURE how far each arm step misses a full turn
Arms qq · toneTurnsDrift
Take q steps and you almost get back where you started. The drift is how far you miss by. A small drift means a tight figure; two arm counts next to each other, like 262 and 263, means the tone sits between two closures and fills in evenly instead of picking a few arms.
Drift is q·tone reduced to the nearest multiple of 360°, signed. A negative drift winds the arms one way and a positive drift the other; the magnitude sets the pitch of the arm as it climbs in polar angle, so the arms read as logarithmic spirals rather than straight rays.

Six samples per period is the floor for a nodal set to read cleanly. Below that the reading says so and the resolution slider is the fix; the stored cells and the arithmetic are unchanged either way.
The surface spacing tops out at 14 π, which the grid can still sample, so a tone below τ = 0.112453 reads at that ceiling — Noble and most of Locked share one surface picture. The stepped shells take the tone directly as degrees with no ceiling, so that form separates the whole τ range and is the one to read the low end on.
