WORKED EXAMPLE · EARTH

Earth is filled with tiny cubes called TL-cells. Each cube takes the density of the rock or metal at its spot, from the standard model seismologists use. Count the cubes, add up their weight, and the total has to match the real Earth.

Each TL-cell of edge h takes the Preliminary Reference Earth Model (PREM) density at its center. Mass, mean density, polar moment of inertia and the gravity profile are sums over cells, compared with the measured values.

SCALE

1 unit = 1 cm (Unreal Engine convention; glTF, Blender and Unity divide by 100 for meters). Geometry is stored as dimensionless TL-cell counts; one declared edge length per scene converts every count to physical units.

One double-precision frame keeps 1 cm out to about 9 × 10¹³ m. Larger scenes give each octree cell its own local origin, so 1 cm holds at any scale.

Teal numbers are within 1% of the measured Earth. The moment of inertia says how much weight sits near the middle: a uniform ball would read 0.400, and Earth reads 0.331 because its heavy iron core is packed in the center.
M = Σ ρᵢh³. ρ̄ = M / (4/3 πR³). I = Σ mᵢ(xᵢ² + yᵢ²). g(r) = G·M(<r)/r². Measured: M = GM/G with GM = 3.986004418 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and G = 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³/(kg·s²); R = 6371.0 km; I/MR² = 0.3307; surface GM/R² = 9.820 m/s².
