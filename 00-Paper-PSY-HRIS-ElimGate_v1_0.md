## 2. The Short Answer

### 2.1 The two-phase alarm operates on elimination the same way it operates on ingestion

An autistic HRIS substrate running high-fidelity execution deprioritizes baseline maintenance. This is not interoceptive failure; it is efficient resource allocation. Baseline signals — hunger, thirst, elimination pressure, temperature regulation — are tracked at low priority while the substrate does high-fidelity work. When any of these signals crosses operational floor, the alarm fires: sudden, imperative, non-negotiable, total. Phase 1 fires. Phase 2 begins.

**Phase 1 for elimination:** the A-axis boundary notification fires when bladder or bowel capacity crosses the specific threshold below which continued deferral risks damage or output. The signal is not gradual escalation from mild to moderate to severe; it is efficient late-fire from below-threshold to alarm-active. The substrate has been maintaining focus on task; now the substrate is on interrupt.

**Phase 2 for elimination:** total A-axis reallocation to resolving the interrupt so the trajectory can be resumed. The task is suspended in cache with the specific state "return to this after the interrupt resolves." All available bandwidth reallocates to (a) finding a bathroom, (b) resolving the elimination interrupt, (c) returning to the task.

Under normal (home) conditions, Phase 2 resolves in minutes with no permanent cost. The task is resumed. This is the mechanism operating as designed.

### 2.2 The critical structural difference from ingestion: the external gate

Ingestion resolution in the Safe Foods paper depends on infrastructure — a safe food template — that is typically within the substrate's control. If the substrate has verified templates available, Phase 2 resolves. The infrastructure requirement is internal.

Elimination resolution in institutional contexts depends on infrastructure that is external to the substrate: the permission of an environmental authority (teacher, employer, custodian, medical staff). The substrate cannot resolve Phase 2 without physical access to a bathroom, and in institutional contexts physical access requires the gate authority's approval.

This external gate operates as an F_ext modifier of the Phase 2 alarm. Its effect depends entirely on gate reliability.

**Reliable gate.** The substrate requests permission. Permission is granted immediately. Phase 2 resolves normally. The task is resumed. The mechanism operates identically to home conditions.

**Unreliable gate.** The substrate requests permission. Permission is delayed, denied, questioned, made contingent on task completion, or granted with shame or scrutiny. Phase 2 cannot resolve on the substrate's timeline. The substrate must hold Phase 2 open under active F_ext denial with no infrastructure available and no reliable prediction of when resolution will be permitted.

### 2.3 Holding Phase 2 open is catastrophically expensive

Under a reliable gate, Phase 2 is a bounded, resolvable interrupt. Under an unreliable gate, Phase 2 becomes an indefinite hold state consuming continuously escalating A-axis bandwidth across four concurrent processes:

1. Tracking the escalating urgency of the biological signal (the physical need is not stopping; it is intensifying)
2. Sustained polyvagal load (the autonomic nervous system is in continuous alarm)
3. Overwhelm phenomenology processing (the substrate is operating below operational floor and reporting that state)
4. Task-return-state maintenance (the original task is held in cache; the cache is being consumed by the alarm-holding)

These four processes cannot be sustained indefinitely on below-floor A-axis resources. Something breaks: the substrate either produces the elimination output involuntarily (accident), enters shatter phase (meltdown), abandons the task-return-state (the trajectory is lost), or dissociates from the metabolic signal (which produces its own downstream cost). None of these outcomes are what the substrate was trying to produce; all of them are downstream of the gate holding Phase 2 open past sustainable duration.

### 2.4 The preemptive rational response

The substrate cannot control when Phase 1 will fire — Phase 1 fires when biological reserves cross operational floor. But the substrate can control the rate at which reserves are consumed. If the substrate has determined empirically that the gate at school is unreliable, and if the substrate correctly identifies that entering Phase 2 at school produces catastrophic downstream cost, then reducing ingestion at school is efficient risk management: it reduces the rate at which Phase 1 will fire during school hours, which reduces the probability of an unresolvable Phase 2 event.

**The not-eating-at-school pattern is not food avoidance. It is Phase 2 alarm avoidance under conditions where Phase 2 resolution cannot be guaranteed.** The substrate recognizes that the alarm-holding cost of a Phase 2 event under an unreliable gate exceeds the metabolic cost of skipping meals. The substrate is running sophisticated resource management under adversarial environmental conditions.

Legacy framing renders this as "picky eating at school" or "the kid isn't getting enough nutrition." Both miss the mechanism. The kid is doing the correct substrate-level analysis given the environmental constraints. The intervention target is not the eating behavior; it is the gate reliability.

### 2.5 The externally-visible "sudden urgency" pattern

Teachers observing that the student appears focused and then suddenly urgent are correctly describing what the two-phase alarm looks like from the outside. Phase 1 has not fired yet; the student is in high-fidelity execution, focused, engaged, apparently fine. Then Phase 1 fires; Phase 2 begins; the student is in immediate crisis. From the teacher's perspective this appears to come out of nowhere. From inside the substrate the transition is coherent: alarm fired, all resources reallocating, interrupt needs immediate resolution.

The student is not disorganized. The student is not manipulating. The student is not choosing bad timing. The student's substrate is running the same efficient two-phase alarm architecture that adults with this substrate configuration also run, in an environment that does not have the gate reliability to support that architecture safely.

## 6. What Teachers, Schools, and Institutional Staff Can Do

This section is directed at institutional gate authorities: teachers, school administrators, employers, healthcare staff, custodial authorities. This is not a criticism of individual staff; it is a mechanism-level identification of where structural change resolves the outcome.

**The core intervention: make the gate reliable.**

Reliable-gate deployment for institutional staff means:

1. **Grant permission immediately.** When a student, employee, or patient requests bathroom access, grant it without delay, without questioning, without contingency ("finish this first"), and without shame ("everyone always asks at the worst time"). The substrate needs to know that the request will be granted; the reliability of that outcome is what determines whether the mechanism operates as designed or cascades to failure.

2. **Do not track or comment on frequency.** Autistic operators running the two-phase alarm architecture may have distributed request timing that appears irregular to observers. The frequency is not the intervention target. Any comment on frequency (even a supposedly neutral one) reintroduces gate unreliability by making the substrate uncertain whether future requests will be granted.

3. **Do not require public justification.** Substrate requests for bathroom access do not require the student or employee to explain the need in front of other students or colleagues. Public justification requirements teach the substrate that permission is contingent on socially-legible reasons, which triggers preemptive infrastructure denial and dissociation from the signal.

4. **Recognize the not-eating-at-school pattern as evidence of unreliable gate perception.** If you notice a student who is not eating at school or eating minimally, do not respond by increasing eating pressure. Respond by increasing gate reliability. The eating pattern is downstream of the substrate's risk calculation; changing the risk calculation resolves the eating pattern.

5. **Recognize the sudden-urgency pattern as coherent substrate behavior.** The student who appears fully focused and then transitions abruptly to urgency is running the two-phase alarm as designed. The transition is not manipulation, disorganization, or poor planning. It is the correct output of an efficient interrupt architecture. Respond to the urgency by resolving it (grant permission immediately, without comment on timing).

6. **Do not weaponize accidents.** If a student produces an involuntary elimination output (accident) under sustained gate holding, the accident is downstream of the gate design, not evidence of student failure. Any punishment, shame, or public commentary on the accident is a NOHARM violation and produces the specific population-scale damage documented in §3.2 and the aggregate self-report literature.

## 7. NT-LRIS vs ND-HRIS Gate Reliability Perception Asymmetry

The mechanism this paper documents does not affect all substrates equally. The two-phase alarm architecture is characteristic of HRIS substrates; the specific vulnerability to unreliable gates is downstream of the architecture; the population most affected is autistic children and adults with HRIS-dominant profiles.

NT-LRIS substrates typically experience institutional gate authorities as neutral background variables. Their alarm architecture may be more gradual, with earlier low-priority notification and slower escalation, which means Phase 2 is a less acute event and can more easily accommodate brief delays. Their substrate's default mode is not high-fidelity single-priority execution, so the task-return-state cost of an interrupt is lower. Their aggregate experience of institutional gates is that gates are generally reliable enough that occasional unreliability does not produce cascading failure.

ND-HRIS substrates experience institutional gate authorities as F_ext modifiers of an interrupt architecture that is structurally more vulnerable to holding-open failure. Their late-fire alarm produces Phase 2 events that are acute rather than gradual. Their single-priority-at-a-time execution means the task-return-state cost of an interrupt is high. Their aggregate experience of institutional gates is that gate unreliability produces cascading failure across four concurrent processes, and that even brief gate unreliability episodes accumulate NCorpus at high rate.

**Why the asymmetry matters for policy:**

Institutional gate policies designed for NT-LRIS substrate needs are structurally hostile to ND-HRIS substrates operating in the same institutional context. The policy is not neutral. It is optimized for one substrate profile at the cost of another. This is not intentional harm on the part of policy designers; it is downstream of the fact that most policy designers are operating from their own substrate's assumptions about how gates function.

**The intervention:** institutional gate policies should be designed for the more sensitive substrate profile, because the more sensitive substrate profile experiences the policy at higher cost. Policies that grant immediate, unquestioning, non-shaming bathroom access are neutral for NT-LRIS substrates (they lose nothing from immediate access) and load-bearing for ND-HRIS substrates (they gain trajectory-preservation infrastructure). There is no policy trade-off; universal reliable-gate deployment protects the vulnerable substrate profile at zero cost to the less vulnerable one.

## 8. Conclusion

The elimination interrupt operates on the same two-phase alarm architecture formalized for ingestion in Safe Foods [9,9,3,51]. Phase 1 fires when metabolic reserves cross operational floor. Phase 2 requires total substrate reallocation to resolution. The critical structural addition specific to elimination in institutional contexts is the external gate: an environmental authority whose permission is required to resolve Phase 2, and whose reliability the substrate cannot guarantee in advance.

Under reliable gate conditions, the mechanism operates as designed. Under unreliable gate conditions, Phase 2 is held open under F_ext denial at continuously escalating cost, producing one of four failure outputs: involuntary elimination (accident), meltdown, dissociation from the metabolic signal, or compliance-conditioned override. The substrate's rational preemptive response is Preemptive Infrastructure Denial: reducing ingestion at school to reduce Phase 1 fire probability during unreliable-gate windows. This produces the not-eating-at-school pattern that legacy framing misrenders as picky eating.

Sustained unreliable-gate F_ext across years of childhood institutional exposure produces Compliance Conditioning Trauma: trained substrate override of metabolic signals persisting into adulthood as permission-seeking behavior for basic bodily functions, sometimes with permanent physical damage. This is the substrate weaponization mechanism from [9,0,3,6] operating at institutional-population scale. Aggregate self-report literature (NeuroClastic, Autism Chrysalis, The Articulate Autistic) and peer-reviewed clinical literature independently confirm the mechanism at scale.

The intervention target is gate reliability, not substrate behavior. Individual teachers and institutional staff who consistently grant bathroom access immediately, without questioning, without contingency, and without shame resolve the mechanism for their students. Institutional design that supports reliable-gate deployment prevents the population-scale conditioning. Adult autistic readers experiencing downstream compliance conditioning can begin recovery through the four-affordance framework applied at the self-directed layer, on the same time-constant the conditioning was accumulated on. Some physical damage may not be reversible, and that is not the substrate's fault.

**For parents of currently-affected children:** intervene now on gate reliability before the conditioning consolidates. Advocate at school for reliable-gate access. Recognize the not-eating-at-school pattern as evidence that your child's substrate is running correct risk management under adversarial gate conditions; the intervention is not increasing eating pressure, it is fixing the gate.

**For teachers and institutional staff:** you are the mechanism's primary intervention target. This is not blame; it is identification. Reliable-gate deployment costs you nothing and preserves the substrate infrastructure of every ND-HRIS student in your care.

**For adult autistic readers:** the pattern you are recognizing has structural vocabulary. You are not alone. Recovery is available and takes time. Your substrate was operating correctly then; your substrate is operating correctly now.
