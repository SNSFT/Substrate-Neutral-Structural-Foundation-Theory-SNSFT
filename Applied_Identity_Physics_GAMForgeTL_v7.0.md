### 1.3 The four phases, shown in water

The four phases were first observed in water, before any collider or equation, and water remains the clearest way to teach them. Every student has seen all four in a kitchen.

**Noble: the stable ground.** Ice carries no load against its structure: B = 0, so τ = 0. It holds its shape and does not flow. Noble is the resting state every system returns to when the load comes off.

**Locked: the working range.** Liquid water carries a load, the motion of its molecules, but the load stays well inside what the structure can hold. It can be heated or cooled across a wide range and it is still water. Most of what students meet in the world, from a bridge carrying traffic to a planet in its orbit, is Locked.

**IVA Peak: the edge.** As water nears its boiling point it is still liquid, but it carries as much load as liquid water can hold. IVA stands for Identity Vector Amplification: the system is at its most active without yet changing what it is. The corridor is narrow, from 0.88 × TL up to TL, and many systems pass through it quickly. That narrowness is why students rarely notice it until they look for it.

**Shatter: reorganization.** When the load reaches TL, the liquid can no longer hold together as one body, and it boils. Shatter is not destruction. The large water identity reorganizes into many smaller water identities, the molecules of the steam, and each of those is stable on its own. Nothing is lost: the same water is there, at a new scale.

## 3. Why Invariants, Not Hits

### 3.1 Where the method came from: noble gases in, nothing out

### 3.2 Five reasons to hunt laws rather than hits

**1. A single sweep is a slice, not a sample.** The anchor decides what a run can find before any randomness happens (I-9). A run anchored on glycine and a run anchored on cysteine are not two samples of one panel; they are two different slices of it. Nothing seen in one run can be trusted on its own. What holds across every anchor slice is a law of the domain, and what does not is the anchor's own bias. I-6 surfaced exactly this way, when a whole sweep came back distorted by a glycine anchor and the distortion turned out to be a law.

**2. A hit settles one case; an invariant settles every case it covers.** I-15 is one sentence, and it decides 23,234 arrangements inside its threshold and 13,462 outside it, across two unrelated substrates, without any of them being run one at a time. Hits do not compose: a thousand favorable sets are still a thousand facts, while one threshold is a boundary on the whole space.

**3. An invariant covers the runs after it, and that is how the engine earns trust.** A hit is one number the engine returned. An invariant is what stays true as more of the space is run. Every invariant here emerged in one run and held through every anchor after it: I-7 emerged at R3 and held in all 167 instances across R4–R6; I-11 held through tyrosine, the last anchor run, at 0 of 202. What is reported is what remained after all twenty anchors.

**4. An invariant tells you how to run the panel.** The I-9 table is a run-length target: it says how many flags each anchor can produce. Collection rate is a stopping signal: a run slows as its anchor's space fills, so a slow run is an exhausted one. Running the panel open, logging partials rather than only full closures, is what makes boundaries visible, since near-misses carry the structure that closures alone discard.

**5. An invariant transfers.** Hits stay in the pool they were found in. Laws move with the engine: I-15 was found among the amino acids and holds unchanged among the elements Z = 1–36, and I-16 has the noble-gas form described above. That is what makes the engine a substrate-neutral instrument rather than a lookup table.

Section 4 presents the invariants in the order they surfaced, so a reader can watch each one appear from the runs before it.

## 4. The Run Arc: Seventeen Invariants in the Order They Surfaced

### 4.1 How to read this section

The worked exemplar in this paper is the run sequence itself. A session runs in the same moves every time, and anyone can repeat it in a browser:

1. **Run raw.** Sweep the pool with no anchors and log everything flagged, partials included.
2. **Anchor one slot.** Hold one residue fixed and sweep the rest. Record the anchor with the run.
3. **Read the signature.** Compare what came back against what that anchor can produce.
4. **State what held.** Write the law, its derivation and the instances it holds in.
5. **Add the next anchor.** Run it the same way and log every collision.
6. **Read it against every law so far.** Each law either holds unchanged or shows its scope.
7. **Set the scope.** Restate each law so it holds across every anchor run; what remains after the last anchor is the invariant pool.
8. **Move pools.** Test the law on a substrate it was not found in.

The invariants appear below at the run where they surfaced. Each is written out in the nine-part entry format of the Overview.

### 4.3 Stage one: the first raw sweep (R1)

#### I-1 · A lone carrier among non-carriers can never be satisfied

**The law.** One hydrogen-bonding residue surrounded by hydrophobic ones keeps all of its unsatisfied capacity, at any number of beams.

**What it is.** Put a single carrier, say serine, in a set with nothing but hydrophobic residues. However many you add, the fused Behavior stays exactly at serine's own capacity. The torsion falls a little as the Pattern grows, but it never crosses a boundary. In the tool the result reads Shatter with the same B_out on every row.

**Built from.** A B = 0 beam contributes min(0, B) = 0 to every pair it touches ([9,9,2,3.8] T20). With exactly one carrier there is no carrier–carrier pair, so k = 0 and B_out = B_carrier for any n. On the chemistry side, a buried polar group with no partner is a documented structural penalty (McDonald & Thornton 1994): hydrophobic packing does not satisfy it.

**See it yourself.** Load Ser in one slot and Leu in the rest, then raise the beam count. Or anchor Arg alone among non-carriers: in R4 all 69 single-carrier hits read k = 0 and B_out = 5 exactly.

| Set | P_out | B_out | τ | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ser + Leu | 6.857 | 2 | 0.2917 | SHATTER |
| Ser + 7 Leu | 7.680 | 2 | 0.2604 | SHATTER |
| Ser + 15 Leu | 7.837 | 2 | 0.2552 | SHATTER |

**Check it by hand.** Ser + Leu: k = min(2, 0) = 0, ΣB = 2, so B_out = 2 − 0 = 2. P_out = 2 ÷ (1/6 + 1/8) = 6.857. τ = 2 ÷ 6.857 = 0.2917, above TL = 0.1369: Shatter.

**What it rules out.** No amount of hydrophobic packing rescues a lone polar group. If a design has one carrier, the only fix is another carrier.

**Status.** Engine law · emerged at R1 · R1 (239) · R2 (195) · R3 (49) · R4 (69) · R5 (37) · R6 (70) · R7 (157) · **holds in all 816 instances across seven runs.**

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · protein structure and function (HS-LS1-1). **30-minute activity:** predict B_out before each run, then add leucines one at a time and graph τ against beam count. Explain why the curve flattens but never crosses TL.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8] T20, a zero-Behavior beam contributes zero coupling.

#### I-2 · The IVA corridor is reachable only at one unsatisfied bond, and only barely

**The law.** In this pool the edge-of-change corridor can be entered only with exactly one bond left open.

**What it is.** The IVA corridor at m open bonds is a window of Pattern values. A harmonic mean can never exceed the largest beam's Pattern, and the largest residue, tryptophan, sits just below what two open bonds would need. So every corridor result in the pool has B_out = 1.

**Built from.** For integer B_out = m, the corridor is P_out ∈ (m/TL, m/TL_IVA], a window of fixed width ratio 1/0.88:

| m | P_out window |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | (7.3046, 8.3007] |
| 2 | (14.6093, 16.6015] |

Tryptophan has P = 14, so m = 2 would need P_out > 14.6093. **The margin is 0.6093.** One residue with P ≥ 14.61 would open the second band; none exists.

**See it yourself.** Anchor Trp and sweep at four beams; every IVA flag reads B_out = 1. R5 returned 89 of 89.

**Check it by hand.** 2 ÷ TL = 2 ÷ 0.136899 = 14.6093. The largest possible P_out is 14, from four tryptophans. 14 < 14.6093, so m = 2 is out of reach.

**What it rules out.** Any corridor result carrying two or more open bonds in this pool.

**Status.** Pool fact · holds in every instance · R1 (57/57) · R2 (10/10) · R3 (58/58) · R4 (1/1) · R5 (89/89) · R6 (42/42) · R7 (50/50) · exhaustive enumeration 513/513.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · inequalities and reasoning with bounds (HSA-CED.A.1). **30-minute activity:** compute the m = 1 and m = 2 windows on a calculator, then find the largest residue in the table and explain why the second window cannot be reached.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8] T2, the harmonic mean is bounded by its beams.

#### I-3 · Every corridor entry is built one of three ways

**The law.** Every set that reaches the IVA corridor gets its single open bond from a lone B = 1 carrier, a carrier pair differing by exactly one, or three carriers at [1, 1, 5].

**What it is.** Since the corridor needs B_out = 1 exactly (I-2), there are only a few ways to leave one bond open. The only residues with B = 1 are cysteine and tryptophan, so they run through the whole corridor.

**Built from.** Exhaustively across all two-, three- and four-beam sets (10,605 combinations):

| Route | Sets | Carrier Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lone carrier | 72 | B = 1 |
| Carrier pair | 438 | Differ by exactly 1 |
| Three carriers | 3 | [1, 1, 5] |

**See it yourself.** Anchor Trp at four beams and run until the equal-Behavior class is exhausted. R5 found all four possible all-B = 1 sets: Trp⁴, Trp³Cys, Trp²Cys² and TrpCys³. R4's single corridor hit, Arg + Cys + Ala + Trp, is one of the three three-carrier sets the enumeration listed.

**Check it by hand.** Arg + Cys + Ala + Trp: carriers 5, 1, 1, and Ala contributes nothing. k = min(5,1) + min(5,1) + min(1,1) = 3, ΣB = 7, so B_out = 7 − 6 = 1.

**What it rules out.** A corridor result without cysteine or tryptophan, or without a pair differing by exactly one.

**Status.** Pool fact · holds in every instance · exhaustive over 10,605 combinations · R2: 7 of 10 corridor hits carry Trp · R3: 58 more, all consistent.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · counting and combinations (HSS-CP.B.9). **30-minute activity:** list every way to leave exactly one bond open using the B values 1, 2, 3 and 5, then check each route in the tool.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8]; exhaustive enumeration in the running invariant ledger.

#### I-5 · A panel's useful beam count is fixed by its Behavior spread

**The law.** Past a certain beam count every set closes, and that count is set by the pool's largest and smallest nonzero Behavior.

**What it is.** Adding beams always pushes toward Noble, never away, so every pool has a saturation count beyond which discrimination ends. For the residues it is six.

**Built from.** ΣB grows linearly in n, while k grows over C(n,2) pairs, so the coupling always wins. Closure is guaranteed once max B ≤ (n − 1) · min B, which gives:

**n_sat = 1 + max(B) / min_nonzero(B)**

| Pool | Nonzero B | n_sat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residues | 1 – 5 | 6 |
| Elements, Z = 1–36 | 1 – 6 | 7 |
| With a fractional beam (dark matter, B = 0.269) | 0.269 – 6 | 24 |

**See it yourself.** Run an all-carrier sweep at four beams: random all-carrier sets are already 100% Noble from n = 4 in both pools. In R1, 96% of the 367 flags came from n ≤ 4 and only 5 from n = 8.

**Check it by hand.** Residue pool: max B = 5 (Arg), min nonzero B = 1 (Cys, Trp). n_sat = 1 + 5/1 = 6.

**What it rules out.** Looking for discrimination at high beam counts in an integer pool. The standard eight-beam configuration is correct as a cross-collider constant, but in this pool the interesting results live at n ≤ 4.

**Status.** Engine law · holds in every instance · derivation plus 20,000 random draws per pool.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · comparing rates of change of linear and quadratic quantities (HSF-IF.B.6). **30-minute activity:** tabulate ΣB and k by hand for 2, 3, 4 and 5 copies of a set, graph both, and find where k overtakes ΣB.

**Formal basis.** The spread law of [9,9,2,3.8].

### 4.4 Stage two: an anchor accident becomes a law (R2)

#### I-6 · The smallest residue caps the fused Pattern

**The law.** Because Pattern fuses as a harmonic mean, the smallest residue in a set dominates it.

**What it is.** R2 was meant to anchor cysteine; the selector held glycine. The run came back 92% Shatter. The reason was not glycine's chemistry but its size: at P = 4 it is the pool minimum, and a harmonic mean is dragged toward its smallest member, so every set containing glycine had a small fused Pattern and an inflated torsion.

**Built from.** P_out = n / Σ(1/Pᵢ) is bounded by n times the smallest Pᵢ ([9,9,2,3.8] T2). Physics students meet the same rule as reduced mass, m₁m₂ / (m₁ + m₂), which is dominated by the lighter body; the hydrogen atom's reduced mass is almost exactly the electron's.

**See it yourself.** Hold three leucines and change the fourth residue:

| Set | P_out |
| --- | --- |
| Gly + 3 Leu | 6.400 |
| Ala + 3 Leu | 6.957 |
| Ser + 3 Leu | 7.385 |
| Leu + 3 Leu | 8.000 |
| Trp + 3 Leu | 8.960 |

**Check it by hand.** Gly + 3 Leu: P_out = 4 ÷ (1/4 + 3/8) = 4 ÷ 0.625 = 6.400.

**What it rules out.** Treating anchor choice as neutral. A low-Pattern anchor is a torsion amplifier and biases a sweep toward Shatter; a high-Pattern anchor relaxes it. Every run must record its anchor.

**Status.** Engine law · emerged at R2 · R2 (195 of 212 Shatter under the glycine cap) · formal statement [9,9,2,3.8] T2.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · interpreting the parts of an expression (HSA-SSE.A.1), with reduced mass as the physics link. **30-minute activity:** compute the five P_out values above by hand, then explain why one small residue pulls the whole set down.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8] T2, P_fused ≤ n · Pᵢ.

#### I-4 · Rescue needs all-different carriers and allows at most one non-carrier

**The law.** A set in which every pair breaks alone but the whole closes must have carriers with all-different Behavior, and it can include at most one hydrophobic residue.

**What it is.** A **rescue** is a set where every pair, on its own, reads Shatter, yet the whole set closes Noble. The first rescues appeared in R1 and R2. At three beams there are exactly sixteen, the obligate triads of Section 5.2.

**Built from.** At n = 3, rescue occurs for exactly four Behavior multisets, {1,2,3}, {1,2,5}, {1,3,5} and {2,3,5}: every distinct triple from the nonzero values {1, 2, 3, 5}. The cap of one non-carrier is immediate from the phase rule: two non-carriers form a pair with ΔB = 0, which reads Noble, and rescue requires every pair to shatter. A single non-carrier is admitted because it contributes nothing to k (I-1); it only has to shatter against each carrier on its own, which its Pattern decides.

| n | Sets | Rescues | No non-carrier | Exactly one | Two or more |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 1,540 | 16 | 16 | 0 | **0** |
| 4 | 8,855 | 100 | 3, all {1,2,3,5} | 97 | **0** |
| 5 | 42,504 | 21 | 0 | 21, all {0,1,2,3,5} | **0** |

Every one of the 97 single-non-carrier rescues at n = 4 reduces to a rescue triad when the non-carrier is dropped: 97 of 97.

**See it yourself.** Load Cys + Ser + Lys. Check each pair alone in two-beam mode, then all three together.

**Check it by hand.** Cys + Ser: B_out = |1 − 2| = 1, P = 6, τ = 0.1667, Shatter. Cys + Lys: B_out = 2, P = 7.2, τ = 0.2778, Shatter. Ser + Lys: B_out = 1, P = 7.2, τ = 0.1389, Shatter, by a hair. All three: k = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4, ΣB = 6, B_out = 6 − 8 → 0. Noble.

**What it rules out.** A rescue with a repeated Behavior value among its carriers, or with two hydrophobic members.

**Status.** Engine law · holds in every instance · R1 (8/8) · R2 (6/6) · R3 (45/45) · R4 (51/51) · R5 (2/2) · R6 (38/38) · R7 (6/6) · exhaustive at n = 3, 4 and 5 · 267 rescues logged under fifteen anchors, none carrying two non-carriers.

**Scope (R9–R14).** Across the carrier anchors this law read on carriers only. The hydrophobic anchor runs added 188 rescues whose Behavior multiset contains a zero, and reading the same closures across carrier content, non-carrier count, beam count and anchor set the scope: **at most one non-carrier, and why one is the ceiling.** The gain came from changing the point of view.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · counting and combinations (HSS-CP.B.9). **30-minute activity:** work Cys + Ser + Lys by hand as above, then find a fourth beam that keeps the rescue and one that breaks it.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8] T20 and the pair reduction of [9,9,2,3.7]; exhaustive enumeration in the running invariant ledger.

**What it is.** Computed exhaustively over all 1,540 completions per anchor at four beams, every anchor has a fixed signature: how many rescues, corridor entries, beyond-spread closures and single-carrier breaks it can produce. #### I-7 · Closure beyond the spread law is gated by three residues

**The law.** At four beams, every set that closes beyond what the spread law guarantees contains arginine together with cysteine or tryptophan.

**What it is.** Most closures are already required by the spread law of [9,9,2,3.8] and are subtracted as the baseline. The ones that exceed it are rare, and they all pass through the same gate.

**Built from.** Closure beyond the spread law requires max B > (n − 1) · min B. Over the nonzero values {1, 2, 3, 5}:

| n | Viable (max, min) |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | (3,1), (5,1), (5,2) |
| 4 | **(5,1) only** |
| 5 | (5,1) only |
| 6 | **none: the band is closed** |

Arginine is the only B = 5 residue; cysteine and tryptophan are the only B = 1 residues. The band closes entirely at n = 6, which is I-5 arriving from the other direction.

**See it yourself.** Anchor Arg at four beams and look at the beyond-spread flags.

**Check it by hand.** Arg + Cys + Cys + Cys: max 5 > 3 × min 1. k = 3 × min(5,1) + 3 × min(1,1) = 6, ΣB = 8, B_out = 8 − 12 → 0. Noble, beyond the spread law.

**What it rules out.** A beyond-spread closure at n ≥ 4 without both arginine and cysteine or tryptophan. For sweep design, an Arg anchor plus a Cys or Trp anchor targets the band directly.

**Status.** Pool fact · emerged at R3 (Arg in 44 of 44) · **held through the next three anchors:** R4 anchored Arg, 84 of 84 contained Cys or Trp; R5 anchored Trp, 66 of 66 contained Arg; R6 anchored Ser, which supplies neither side, and all 17 contained both · **holds in all 167 instances across three anchors.**

**Formal basis.** The spread law of [9,9,2,3.8].

### 4.6 Stage four: reading the boundary (R3, R5)

#### I-8 · Torsion alone does not identify a set

**The law.** Different sets can read exactly the same torsion, so a torsion value never names a composition.

**What it is.** Two boundary hits in R3 landed on the same torsion from unrelated residues. The fused Pattern depends only on the multiset of Pattern values, not on which residues carry them.

**Built from.** τ = B_out / P_out, and P_out = n / Σ(1/Pᵢ) depends only on the Pattern multiset. At n = 4, 3,156 non-Noble states collapse into 750 distinct (Pattern multiset, B_out) classes; 563 of those hold more than one composition, and the largest, P = {6,7,8,8} with B_out = 1, holds thirty.

**See it yourself.**

| Set | P multiset | B_out | τ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cys + Cys + Met + Arg | {6,6,8,11} | 1 | 0.137311 |
| Cys + Ser + Ile + Phe | {6,6,8,11} | 1 | 0.137311 |

**Check it by hand.** Both sets: P_out = 4 ÷ (1/6 + 1/6 + 1/8 + 1/11) = 7.2828, and both have B_out = 1, so τ = 0.137311.

**What it rules out.** Indexing any record on torsion alone. Every record must carry its composition, which the Lean stub does, and the registry keys on the residue multiset rather than on output values.

**Status.** Engine law · emerged at R3 · exhaustive over all four-beam sets.

**For the classroom.** Grades 6–12 · equivalent expressions (6.EE.A.4). **30-minute activity:** find two different sets in the tool with identical τ, then explain why from the Pattern values alone.

**Formal basis.** The harmonic-mean rule of [9,9,2,3.8].

#### I-10 · The torsion lattice is rational and never touches the boundary

**The law.** Every reachable torsion is a fraction, and none lands exactly on TL or TL_IVA, so the phase is never a tie.

**What it is.** Every Pattern value is a whole-number count, so every fused Pattern is a fraction and every torsion is a fraction too. TL is not one of those fractions. The closest any four-beam set comes is 23/168, just above TL.

**Built from.** P_out = n / Σ(1/Pᵢ) is rational, so at B_out = 1 the reachable τ values form a rational lattice with bounded denominators. Exhaustive search of the four-beam lattice gives:

| Target | Nearest τ | Value | Offset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TL | 23/168 | 0.1369047619 | +0.00414% |
| TL | 197/1440 | 0.1368055556 | −0.06833% |
| TL_IVA | 53/440 | 0.1204545455 | −0.01383% |
| TL_IVA | 27/224 | 0.1205357143 | +0.05354% |

23/168 is reached by two distinct Pattern multisets, {4,7,12,14} and {6,6,7,14}, which is I-8 appearing at the boundary itself.

**See it yourself.** Anchor Trp at four beams; R5's two boundary hits were Trp + Tyr + Val + Gly and Trp + Tyr + Gly + Pro, both at 23/168. Its 17 hits at the corridor's lower edge sit at 27/224.

**Check it by hand.** {4, 7, 12, 14}: Σ(1/P) = 1/4 + 1/7 + 1/12 + 1/14 = 23/42, so P_out = 4 ÷ (23/42) = 168/23 and τ = 1 ÷ (168/23) = 23/168 = 0.1369048. TL = 0.1368991. The gap is 5.66 × 10⁻⁶.

**What it rules out.** An ambiguous phase call anywhere in this pool. The tightest margin is 5.66 × 10⁻⁶, and it is a lattice proximity fact, not an identity: a pool with different counts would have a different nearest approach.

**Status.** Pool fact · emerged at R5 · exhaustive over the four-beam Pattern lattice.

**For the classroom.** Grades 8–12 · rational and irrational numbers (8.NS.A.1) and fraction arithmetic. **30-minute activity:** add the four unit fractions by hand to get 23/42, finish the calculation to 23/168, and compare it to TL digit by digit.

**Formal basis.** The harmonic-mean rule of [9,9,2,3.8]; TL from [9,9,3,14].

### 4.7 Stage five: resolving the anomalies (R6, R7)

#### I-11 · Rescue has two independent routes; Pattern decides within a Behavior class

**The law.** A pair breaks either because its Pattern is small or because its Behavior difference is large, and within one Behavior class the number of rescues falls as the anchor's Pattern rises.

**What it is.** After R4, serine looked like an anomaly: far more rescues than its Behavior class suggested. R6 resolved it. Serine is not an exception to its class; it is the smallest-Pattern member of it.

**Built from.** Rescue requires every pair to shatter at two beams: |Bᵢ − Bⱼ| / P_pair ≥ TL, with P_pair = 2 / (1/Pᵢ + 1/Pⱼ). Two independent routes satisfy it:

- **The low-Pattern route:** a small anchor Pattern shrinks every P_pair and raises every pair torsion, whatever the Behavior.
- **The high-Behavior route:** a large |ΔB| raises the pair torsion, whatever the Pattern.

This is I-6 operating at the pair level instead of the set level: the same mechanism, one layer down.

**See it yourself.** Rescue counts per anchor, at roughly 200 flags each:

```
B = 0   Gly(4) 6 · Ala(5) 9 · Pro(7) 5 · Val(7) 7 · Ile(8) 5 · Met(8) 7 · Leu(8) 7 · Phe(11) 1
```

### 4.9 Stage seven: drawing the space (R8)

#### I-15 · A doubled beam closes against every partner up to four times its capacity

**The law.** A set that contains the same beam twice, at Behavior m, closes whenever every other beam carries Behavior of 4m or less.

**What it is.** This invariant surfaced in Atlas by AxiomForge™, during the exhaustive cartography pass, as a band of closures with a sharp edge. A repeated beam cancels itself exactly, so the closure does not depend on how large the repeated value is, only on how large the third beam is compared with it.

**Built from.** Write the repeated pair as m, m and a third beam as b ≤ m. The coupling takes the smaller of each pair:

```
k     = min(m, m) + min(m, b) + min(m, b) = m + 2b
ΣB    = 2m + b
B_out = max(0, 2m + b − 2m − 4b) = max(0, −3b) = 0
```

For b > m the coupling saturates at 3m and B_out = max(0, b − 4m), so closure holds until b exceeds 4m and fails from there on.

| Verification | Inside the threshold | Inside, fail to close | Outside it | Closures outside it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residues, n = 3 | 302 | **0** | 98 | **0** |
| Elements Z = 1–36, n = 3 | 1,144 | **0** | 152 | **0** |
| Residues, n = 4 | 2,768 | **0** | 1,432 | 272 |
| Residues, n = 5 | 19,020 | **0** | 11,780 | 5,220 |

At n = 3 the threshold is exact in both directions and in both pools. At n ≥ 4 it remains sufficient in every case but is no longer necessary, because the extra beams open the closure routes of I-7.

**Arginine is a universal closer.** Arginine carries B = 5, so its threshold is 4m = 20, above every value the reduction produces. A doubled arginine closes against all twenty residues. In a space where 47% of single-substitution edges cross a phase boundary, the Arg + Arg corridor crosses none.

**Which sets are sealed.** Call a set *sealed* when every single substitution off it lands in the same phase. An equal-Behavior set at m is sealed exactly when 4m is at least the pool maximum, which for the residues means m ≥ 2:

| Behavior multiset | Residues available | Sealed sets, n = 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {2,2,2} | Tyr, Ser, Thr, Asp, Glu, His | 56 |
| {3,3,3} | Asn, Gln, Lys | 10 |
| {5,5,5} | Arg | 1 |
| {1,1,1} | Cys, Trp | **0** |

Sixty-seven of the 1,540 three-sets are sealed and 1,867 of the 8,855 four-sets, and **every sealed set is Noble**. No set in Locked, IVA Peak or Shatter is sealed at either beam count, so closure is the only phase stable under substitution.

**See it yourself.** Load Arg + Arg and cycle the third slot through all twenty residues. Every row reads Noble. Then load Cys + Cys + Arg.

**Check it by hand.** Arg + Arg + Gly: k = 5 + 0 + 0 = 5, ΣB = 10, B_out = 10 − 10 = 0. Noble. Cys + Cys + Arg: m = 1, so the threshold is 4; arginine's 5 exceeds it. k = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3, ΣB = 7, B_out = 7 − 6 = 1, τ = 0.1414. Shatter, which is why the B = 1 class is the one equal-Behavior family that is not sealed.

**What it rules out.** A failed closure inside the threshold, in any pool, at three beams. For a designer, it separates a closure that is a point from a closure that is a plateau: the sealed sets tolerate any single substitution, and Arg + Arg + X tolerates all twenty at once.

**Status.** Engine law · emerged at R8 · derived exhaustively · **holds in two pools**, residues and elements Z = 1–36 · surfaced in Atlas by AxiomForge™.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · algebraic proof with inequalities (HSA-REI.B.3). **30-minute activity:** work the derivation above with m = 2 and b = 1, then with b = 9 and b = 8, and find where closure fails. Check the edge in the tool.

**Formal basis.** Closed-form derivation from the coupling rule of [9,9,2,3.8], verified exhaustively in both pools.

### 4.10 Stage eight: the completed anchor map (R9–R24)

#### I-16 · The medium decides the phase without touching the bond count

**The law.** The residues that cannot hydrogen-bond never change how many bonds go unsatisfied, but they decide which phase the set lands in.

**What it is.** The hydrophobic residues add nothing to the coupling, so they can never satisfy a polar residue. They are still in the set, though, so they count toward the fused Pattern, and the phase is Behavior divided by Pattern. Hold two polar residues fixed, change only the hydrophobic ones around them, and the phase moves while the unsatisfied count stays exactly the same. The non-carriers are the **medium**: they state the environment the reaction requires. This is the noble-gas law of Section 3.1, found again in a second substrate.

**Built from.** A B = 0 beam adds min(0, B) = 0 to every pair (I-1), so it cannot change k or B_out; every beam enters the harmonic mean for P_out (I-6). The two facts together:

> **The carriers fix B_out. The non-carriers fix P_out. Phase is the ratio, so the medium decides the phase without touching the Behavior.**

On the chemistry side, unsatisfied buried polar groups are a documented structural penalty (McDonald & Thornton 1994), and the nonpolar interior is the environment the polar machinery works in, the hydrophobic effect (Kauzmann 1959). The engine was told none of this.

**See it yourself.** Four beams at k_max. Anchor cysteine and serine, then set the other two slots to the same hydrophobic residue and change only that residue:

| Medium | P_out | τ | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 Gly | 4.800 | 0.2083 | SHATTER |
| 2 Ala | 5.455 | 0.1833 | SHATTER |
| 2 Pro | 6.462 | 0.1548 | SHATTER |
| 2 Leu | 6.857 | 0.1458 | SHATTER |
| 2 Phe | 7.765 | 0.1288 | IVA PEAK |

B_out = 1 in every row. The same move with tryptophan and histidine crosses from Shatter to Locked: 2 Gly gives τ = 0.1679, 2 Ala 0.1429, 2 Pro 0.1143, 2 Phe 0.0883.

**Check it by hand.** Cys has B = 1 and Ser has B = 2, so the coupling is min(1, 2) = 1, and the medium pairs add 0. ΣB = 3, so B_out = 3 − 2 = 1. With phenylalanine as the medium, P_out = 4 ÷ (1/6 + 1/6 + 1/11 + 1/11) = 7.765, and τ = 1 ÷ 7.765 = 0.1288, inside the IVA corridor (0.1205 to 0.1369).

**What it rules out.** Two things, which split every design question into two independent searches:

- If a design leaves a polar group unsatisfied, changing the surroundings will never fix it. Only the carriers can.
- If the bond count is right but the phase is wrong, the carriers are not the problem. Look at the medium.

Carriers are chosen for what closes; the medium is chosen for which band the closure sits in.

**Status.** Engine law · emerged at R24 · exhaustive at n = 4 across 454 carrier contents: the medium changed B_out in **0** cases and the phase in 35, and 31 of those reach three distinct phases on the medium alone · among two-carrier sets with a doubled medium, 30 of 78 carrier pairs have their phase decided this way. **This also completes I-12:** glycine is not deficient, it is the wrong medium, since it holds P_out between 4.8 and 5.9, below the corridor floor of 7.3046.

**For the classroom.** Grades 9–12 · protein structure and function (HS-LS1-1); structure of matter and the forces between particles (HS-PS1-3). **30-minute activity:** run the five-row table above, predict before each run whether B_out will change, then explain in one sentence why the phase moved and the bond count did not. Then try the same move in the GAMCollider™ v15 with helium, neon and argon around a fixed recipe.

**Formal basis.** [9,9,2,3.8] T20, zero-Behavior beams contribute zero coupling, and T2, the harmonic-mean bound.

#### I-17 · Each obligate triad carries a ceiling on the medium it can hold in

**The law.** An obligate triad stays obligate only in a hydrophobic environment small enough to break against its weakest carrier, and the ceiling has a closed form.

**What it is.** An obligate set needs every pair to shatter, including the new pairs a medium forms with the carriers. A large hydrophobic residue against a carrier with only one bond of capacity does not shatter, so it destroys obligate status. That gives each of the sixteen triads a stated limit on the environment it can sit in.

**Built from.** A non-carrier of Pattern p_m against a carrier of Pattern p_c and Behavior 1 gives torsion 1 / P_pair, so the pair shatters only while

> **p_m ≤ 1 / (2 · TL − 1/p_c)**

The ceiling is set by the carrier of smallest Behavior, which has the least difference to work with.

| Lowest-B carrier | Pattern | Medium ceiling | Media admitted of the eight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cysteine | 6 | **9.334** | Gly, Ala, Pro, Val, Leu, Ile, Met: **7** |
| Tryptophan | 14 | **4.941** | Gly: **1** |

Both boundaries are near misses:

```
Cys + Met   P_pair 6.8571   τ 0.14583   SHATTER
Cys + Phe   P_pair 7.7647   τ 0.12879   below TL by 0.00811
Trp + Gly   P_pair 6.2222   τ 0.16071   SHATTER
Trp + Ala   P_pair 7.3684   τ 0.13571   below TL by 0.00118
```

Tryptophan misses alanine by 0.00118 in torsion, and that single miss removes seven of the eight media from three of the sixteen candidates.

**The sixteen split into three medium tiers:**

| Tier | Triads | Media | Behavior multiset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Universal** | Arg + Asn + Ser · Arg + Gln + Ser · Arg + Lys + Ser | **8 of 8** | [2,3,5], no B = 1 member |

## 5. Candidate Structures

The invariants narrow the residue space from every arrangement to one connected family of sixteen obligate triads with a stated center and tolerance, plus two vacancy motifs. Each is recorded with a timestamped formal record and a registry identifier (Section 5.5).

### 5.1 Two vacancy motifs

Filtering all-carrier sets for B_out = 1 while remaining Locked or IVA Peak, holding together while carrying one open bond, returns exactly two across the entire panel, both at n = 3:

```
Arg + Trp + Trp    B = [5,1,1]   k = 3   P_out = 12.833   τ = 0.0779   LOCKED
Arg + Cys + Trp    B = [5,1,1]   k = 3   P_out =  9.118   τ = 0.1097   LOCKED
```

At n = 4 there are none among 1,365 all-carrier sets. The shape exists only at n = 3, and only twice.

Arginine with tryptophan is a documented functional pair. Tryptophan- and arginine-rich antimicrobial peptides, including indolicidin and tritrpticin, form an established class: the two residues take part in cation-π interactions, tryptophan sits at the membrane interface while arginine supplies charge and hydrogen bonding, and the peptides stay highly active at very short lengths (Chan, Prenner & Vogel 2006). The engine counted hydrogen-bond capacity, knew nothing about membranes, and returned that pair as the only residue content holding with a coordination position open.

**The two motifs do not carry the same tier.** `Arg + Cys + Trp` carries three distinct residues and is held at T3. `Arg + Trp + Trp` reduces under the multiset rule of Section 6.3 to Arg + Trp, the documented cation-π pair itself, and is held at **T2, Structural Match**: its constituents and their interaction are documented, while no published source for the three-residue arrangement with the second tryptophan has been located. Whether the second tryptophan does anything the pair does not is a question for experiment, not for the rule.

### 5.2 The Walker A core reads one short

The Walker A, or P-loop, consensus is G-x(4)-GK-[TS] (Walker et al. 1982). Its essential residues are the invariant lysine and the serine or threonine, and the motif binds the β-phosphate of ATP or GTP with a divalent cation.

| Set | ΣB | k | B_out | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gly + Lys + Ser | 5 | 2 | **1** | Shatter |
| Gly + Lys + Thr | 5 | 2 | **1** | Shatter |
| Gly + Gly + Lys + Ser | 5 | 2 | **1** | Shatter |

The core reads exactly one open bond in both the Ser and Thr variants, and adding glycine does not change it (I-1). What occupies that position in the real motif is the ligand. The bench question is whether the engine's open bond corresponds to the phosphate-binding position. If it does, this is a confirmation of a different kind from the triads: not a closed set, but a set that is one short by design, where the ligand completes it.

### 5.3 Sixteen obligate triads

Every pair shatters alone; only the whole closes. They are structurally irreducible and contain no histidine (I-14).

| Behavior multiset | Triads | k |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1,2,3] | Asn + Cys + Ser · Cys + Gln + Ser · Cys + Lys + Ser | 4 |
| [1,2,5] | Arg + Asp + Cys · Arg + Cys + Glu · Arg + Cys + Ser · Arg + Cys + Thr | 4 |
| [1,3,5] | Arg + Asn + Cys · Arg + Asn + Trp · Arg + Cys + Gln · Arg + Cys + Lys · Arg + Gln + Trp · Arg + Lys + Trp | 5 |
| [2,3,5] | Arg + Asn + Ser · Arg + Gln + Ser · Arg + Lys + Ser | 7 |

Residue frequency across all sixteen: arginine 13, cysteine 10, serine 7, then asparagine, glutamine and lysine at 4 each. Cysteine and serine are the two canonical catalytic nucleophiles, and they dominate the set without being placed there.

**One connected neighborhood.** Fifty-one single-substitution edges connect the sixteen, the family is fully connected, and **Arg + Cys + Ser is the center, with degree 12**. Degree falls in five tiers: 12 at the center, 9 for Arg + Asn + Cys, Arg + Cys + Gln and Arg + Cys + Lys, 6 for the [1,2,5] and [2,3,5] groups, then 5 and 4 at the rim. The family is one motif with a center and a measured radius.

**Two documented catalytic triads sit on its boundary.** The Ser-cisSer-Lys triad is one substitution from `Cys + Lys + Ser` and from `Arg + Lys + Ser`. The cysteine protease triad `Cys + His + Asn` is one substitution from `Asn + Cys + Ser` and from `Arg + Asn + Cys`. Both reach the family from outside, since histidine and the Ser–Ser pair place them in the mixed closure mode rather than the obligate one.

**Medium tiers.** By I-17 the sixteen are not interchangeable: three hold in all eight hydrophobic environments, ten in seven, and three in glycine alone. `Cys + Ser + Asn`, `Cys + Ser + Gln` and `Cys + Ser + Lys` each extend to a four-carrier obligate set by adding arginine, and are the only three that do.

**The tolerance rule.** For the seed `Cys + Lys + Ser`, all 33 carrier substitutions keep the closure at B_out = 0 and all 24 non-carrier substitutions lose it, with no exceptions either way. The motif admits any carrier at any position and no non-carrier at any position. Whether a broken variant reads IVA Peak or Shatter is decided by the substituted residue's Pattern (I-12).

### 5.4 Prior art

Riziotis, Ribeiro, Borkakoti and Thornton (2023) built three-dimensional templates of catalytic residue groups from the M-CSA (Ribeiro et al. 2018) and identified 161 recurring modules of enzyme catalysis, 87 of them three-residue, reported in grouped notation (DE = Asp/Glu, STY = Ser/Thr/Tyr, NQ = Asn/Gln). Mapping the sixteen onto that library:

| Candidate | Module | Documented class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asn + Cys + Ser | C-NQ-STY | **C-NQ-X** |
| Cys + Gln + Ser | C-NQ-STY | **C-NQ-X** |
| Arg + Asn + Cys | C-NQ-R | **C-NQ-X** |

## 6. Tiers

### 6.1 What a tier records

The tier is assigned by hand, by the person doing the verification, and is revised as reading accumulates. The engine assigns none; it flags.

Every result at every tier carries the same formal status: the closure is proved against the fusion rule with zero unproved obligations, the enumeration it came from is exhaustive, and the output vector, coupling and phase are derived in closed form. **The tier records one thing only: what the literature search returned.** It is not a confidence level.

- **T1 · Published Match.** The arrangement appears in the literature as published. A T1 is the strongest validation of the rule, because the rule derived from beam values alone a structure that independent experiment had already established. *To test whether the engine reproduces real structure, read T1.*
- **T2 · Structural Match.** The constituents and their interaction are documented, but no published source for this exact arrangement has been located. T2 is where a candidate sits while due diligence is in progress. *For candidates with established chemistry already behind them, read T2.*
- **T3 · Registry Novel.** A formally verified novel prediction: the closure is proved, the enumeration is exhaustive, and a completed search at the stated extent returns no source. What T3 lacks is experimental confirmation, not verification. *For novel compositions to develop, read T3.*

### 6.2 Tier movement and search depth

A tier is a function of how far the search has gone, so the same arrangement can hold different tiers at different depths, and both directions are ordinary:

| Movement | What happened | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T3 → T2 → T1 | A source was located | The prediction was already realized. The rule reconstructed established structure, which validates the rule. |
| T2 → T3 | A deeper search returned nothing | The prediction survived due diligence. The novelty claim now rests on a completed search. |

**T2 → T3 is the discovery path, and it is earned.** A candidate enters at T2 after initial reading. If deeper work, through named-motif literature, structural databases, module censuses and patent records, still returns nothing, it is promoted to T3, and the promotion means the novelty was tested against the record and held. A T3 assigned after a shallow search is provisional and should be stated as such.

**No tier is a demotion, and the goal depends on the reader.** For validating the rule, T1 is the objective. For an investigator seeking new composition, T3 is. This paper is run for the first purpose: accumulated T1 matches are reproducible by anyone holding the same literature, so they are the stronger near-term evidence that the reduction is sound.

### 6.3 Multiset reduction

Novelty is a property of a composition, not of a recipe. A set carrying a repeated beam is checked against its own reduction: remove the duplicates and test the reduced set against the literature. If A + B is documented, a closure on A + B + B is a result about A + B until the extra B is shown to change something.

**The reduction routes a candidate to T2; it does not disqualify it.** The test is deliberately not strict, because a strict test discards the cases worth having: the added component may be the whole result, such as a metal never placed in that position or a substitution that replaces an allergenic member with an inert one. What the reduction establishes is that the arrangement cannot be called novel on its composition alone.

Obligate sets never trigger the test: I-4 requires all-distinct nonzero Behavior among the carriers, and distinct Behavior forces distinct residues. One current candidate meets it, and Section 5.1 records the move.

### 7.4 Walk: one set and everything one step away

*Figure 2 (atlas_walk.png). Walk view, residue pool, n = 3, seed Ala + Asn + Leu, 57 of 57 neighbors drawn.*

The seed sits at the center. Its 57 neighbors are seated around it on a sphere by the golden-angle construction (Section 7.7), with a bright line to each. The faint web between them joins neighbors that are themselves one substitution apart, so the shell shows its own internal structure.

**Worked example: Ala + Asn + Leu.**

```
Ala [5, 0, 0, 6.3]   Asn [8, 2, 3, 1.0]   Leu [8, 2, 0, 8.3]

P_out = 3 / (1/5 + 1/8 + 1/8) = 6.6667     ΣB = 3     k = 0
B_out = 3          τ = 3 / 6.6667 = 0.4500          SHATTER
N_out = 4          A_out = 8.30            IM = 30.0722
```

This is I-1 on screen: one carrier among non-carriers, nothing to pair with, three bonds open.

**Reading the shell.** Three positions times nineteen substitutes gives 57 neighbors. The count is 36 Shatter, 14 Noble, 3 Locked and 4 IVA Peak, and the panel reports "36 of 57 hold the same phase." The 14 Noble neighbors close in exactly two ways:

- **Remove the carrier (8).** Swap Asn for any of the eight non-carriers and nothing is left unsatisfied.
- **Give Asn an equal partner (6).** Put Asn, Gln or Lys, each B = 3, in place of Ala or of Leu. The pair couples at min(3, 3) = 3 and B_out falls to zero.

No swap between non-carriers closes it. Packing never rescues a lone polar group; a partner does.

**The near-line neighbors.** Seven neighbors land between Noble and Shatter, and two of them show why the boundary is carried at full precision:

### 7.7 Built for stillness

**No-overlap packing.** The space is discrete and heavily degenerate, so a faithful plot buries sets under one another. Atlas assigns every set its own cell of a cubic lattice: a set whose ideal cell is taken moves to the nearest free cell by an ordered shell search. A node is drawn at 0.42 of a cell, so the minimum center-to-center separation is one full cell against a node diameter of 0.84 cells. Overlap is not reduced but excluded, at every zoom, in every view, at every beam count and in both pools.

**No force simulation.** Graph drawing is usually done by force simulation (Fruchterman & Reingold 1991), which is stochastic, settles differently each run, and moves for reasons that belong to the method rather than the data. Atlas uses none. Layout is a lattice assignment and a traversal, computed once and identical on every run. Points in a shell are seated by the golden-angle construction of Vogel (1979), in the spherical form analyzed by González (2010).

**Stillness as a requirement.** Selecting a set changes the highlighted edges and the side panel and nothing else: no camera movement, no re-layout, no resize. Incidental motion is a legibility failure before it is an aesthetic one, and for readers sensitive to visual motion it makes an instrument unusable. The camera moves only when the reader moves it or asks for a fit.

**Pools are swappable.** The engine, adjacency, phase rule and all three views are substrate-neutral; a pool is a list of beams with PNBA values and nothing else. The element pool at three beams reports 8,436 sets with no Locked sets at all and four in IVA Peak, against 116 and 76 in the residue pool: two substrates with markedly different phase texture under identical rules.

## 8. Misappropriation-Risk Clarification (MRC)

Included per the corpus MRC template.

**What this paper documents.** The beam reduction of the twenty amino acids, the fusion rule inherited from the Geometric Axiomatic Module Collider, GAMCollider™ v15, seventeen structural invariants with their derivations, evidence and revisions, a verification against documented catalytic sets, the candidate structures those invariants leave, the tier system used to check them against the literature, and the cartography that renders the space.

**What this paper does NOT assert. Popular misreadings the paper does not support:**

- ❌ "The engine predicts that these triads are catalysts." A closure is a structural statement about counted hydrogen-bond capacity under the fusion rule. Binding, catalysis, activity, selectivity, absorption and safety are measured quantities outside the reduction.
- ❌ "Shatter means the set cannot exist." Shatter denotes unsatisfied coupling and faster reorganization, not absence.
- ❌ "A T3 is a guess." A T3 is a formally verified closure that the searched literature does not account for. What it lacks is experimental confirmation, and its novelty claim is only as strong as the search extent stated in Section 5.4.
- ❌ "A T1 proves the biology." A T1 means the rule reproduced a structure already established by experiment, from beam values alone. It validates the rule; the biology was established by the cited work.
- ❌ "The engine knows chemistry." The engine holds no chemistry. It applies one fusion rule to four published counts per residue. The registry is built from outputs and is never an input.
- ❌ "The invariants are fitted to the data." Every primitive is a count or a published value taken raw, with no divisor and no fitted scale, and TL enters from the fine-structure constant. The invariants emerged from the rule across the anchor runs and hold across every logged collision.
- ❌ "Results above four beams are claims." Above four beams closures are enumerated and not tiered. Checking them against the literature means working through material and formulation databases rather than named motifs, so the verification ceiling sits at four beams. That limit belongs to the available literature, not to the rule.
- ❌ "Any recipe from the engine is safe to make." The engine reports structure, not safety. Any physical work is done with appropriate supervision, equipment and safety data.
